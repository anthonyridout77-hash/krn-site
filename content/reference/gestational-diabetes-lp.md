## Why does gestational diabetes happen?

 During pregnancy, hormones from the placenta make insulin work less effectively (insulin resistance). For some women the body cannot make enough extra insulin to keep up, so blood glucose levels rise. It is not caused by anything you did wrong.

### How is it diagnosed?

Most women are tested between 24 and 28 weeks with an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT), the sweet drink test. Some are tested earlier if they have risk factors.

### Why managing it matters

Keeping glucose within your targets supports a healthy pregnancy for you and your baby. Food is one of the most practical tools you have, and small, steady changes usually make the biggest difference. GDM usually resolves after birth, and good habits now also support your long-term health.
