## Why is good nutrition important for a healthy pregnancy?

The nutrients we get from food are the building blocks that support life and overall health. The way we eat day to day has a powerful impact on how we feel, shaping our energy levels, strength, mood, immune system and our ability to maintain a healthy weight. Over time, our diet also plays a big role in reducing the risk of chronic conditions like heart disease, diabetes and certain cancers.
When it comes to pregnancy, nutrition becomes even more important. The foods and drinks you choose before, during and after pregnancy can directly influence your body’s ability to conceive, carry your baby to term and support your little one’s growth and development. This is why having the right guidance at each stage makes such a difference, not only for your health but also for your baby’s future.

That is why it is so important to nourish your body with the nutrients it needs throughout your pregnancy journey. By giving your body the right fuel, you give it the best chance to support you as a mum while also helping your baby grow and thrive.
Dietetics is the science of understanding how food and nutrition work together to influence the human body. It combines evidence-based research with practical knowledge, making it possible to provide clear, reliable guidance that supports both immediate health and long-term wellbeing.

### Nutritionist or Dietitian?
In Australia there is a distinction made between dietitians and other occupations in the nutrition and food science field, including that of a nutritionist.
A nutritionist may be a tertiary qualified nutrition professional that has the expertise to provide a range of evidence-based nutrition services related to nutrition, public health nutrition, policy and research, and community health. It’s important to note that the term ‘nutritionist’ is not regulated. Therefore, this title may be used by dietitians, nutrition scientists and nutrition graduates – and also those with very limited qualifications in nutrition.
Dietitians are also qualified to provide this range of evidence-based nutrition services, but in addition, dietitians have the expertise to provide individual dietary counselling, medical nutrition therapy, group dietary therapy and food service management. A dietitian has undertaken a course of study that included substantial theory and supervised and assessed professional practice in clinical nutrition, medical nutrition therapy and food service management. A detailed list of the knowledge, skills and attributes of dietitians in Australia can be found in the National Competency Standards for Dietitians.

#### Why should I see an Accredited Practicing Dietitian?
Accredited Practising Dietitians (APDs) are university-qualified professionals that undertake ongoing training and education programs to ensure that they are your most up-to-date and credible source of nutrition information. They translate scientific health and nutrition information into practical advice, and practise in line with DAA Professional Standards, including the DAA Code of Professional Conduct and Statement of Ethical Practice.

## Gestational Diabetes (GDM) Nutrition Support
Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is one of the most common pregnancy complications, affecting around 1 in 8 Australian pregnancies. Good news: personalised nutrition is the first-line treatment. As an Accredited Practising Dietitian, Kat works alongside your obstetric team to help you manage blood glucose levels through food, understand your carbohydrate portions, and build a meal plan that keeps both you and your baby nourished throughout pregnancy. Early dietary intervention can reduce the need for insulin and support a healthier birth outcome for you and your little one.

## Medicare Rebates for Dietitian Services
As an Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD), Kat's services may be covered by Medicare through a GP Management Plan (also known as a Chronic Disease Management Plan) or Team Care Arrangement. Eligible clients can claim up to 5 Medicare-rebated sessions per calendar year. Private health insurance may also cover additional sessions depending on your policy. Ask your GP today whether you qualify — it's a great way to access expert pregnancy nutrition support with reduced out-of-pocket costs.
