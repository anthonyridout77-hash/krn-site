## Feel confident and supported as you prepare for IVF and fertility treatments with Kat Ridout by your side.

If you are struggling to conceive, you are not alone. Around one in six couples need extra support on their journey to pregnancy.
For women who are ready to start a family or add another child, and who have experienced fertility or medical challenges, IVF (in vitro fertilisation) is often one of the options that can help make having a biological child possible. Other treatments may include ovulation induction, assisted insemination (IUI) or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).
During IVF, an egg is retrieved and fertilised with sperm in a laboratory to create an embryo. This embryo is then transferred into the uterus, where the hope is that it will implant and grow into a healthy pregnancy. The process is closely supported by medical specialists, who monitor progress with blood tests and ultrasounds.
Alongside this medical care, the right nutrition can make a meaningful difference. With guidance from Kat Ridout, you can prepare your body for IVF, support your fertility and create the best possible environment for your baby to grow. Kat combines evidence-based nutrition with compassionate support to help you feel confident and cared for throughout your journey.

### The lead up to IVF treatment
In the month before beginning your IVF cycle, nourishing your body with a healthy, balanced and personalised diet can help you feel as strong and prepared as possible. This time is an opportunity to gently set the foundations for the process ahead.
It is also normal to experience many different emotions during this stage. Feelings of sadness, anxiety or uncertainty can be common before, during and after IVF. The journey can be physically and emotionally demanding, and it is important to remember you do not have to go through it alone. With the right guidance and support, this time can feel more manageable and positive.

### What to eat during IVF
In the lead up to and during an IVF cycle, good nutrition plays a vital role for both men and women. Supporting sperm health and egg health through diet can make a real difference in giving the process the best chance of success.
Rather than making sudden or extreme changes, it is best to focus on nourishing, sustainable choices. For many couples, following a Mediterranean-style diet rich in whole foods, healthy fats and plant-based nutrients provides an excellent foundation. The exact nutrients needed will vary from person to person, which is why personalised advice is so valuable.
Kat Ridout works with individuals and couples to create realistic, evidence-based nutrition plans that support both fertility and wellbeing throughout the IVF journey. Her goal is to help you feel confident, cared for and empowered every step of the way.
For those looking to conceive, it is important to:

- Include healthy fats such as avocado, nuts, seeds and olive oil
- Eat a variety of leafy greens and colourful vegetables
- Include a source of protein with every meal
- Stay well hydrated by drinking plenty of water
- Limit caffeine and avoid alcohol where possible
- Take a daily prenatal multivitamin supplement
- Manage any existing dietary or medical conditions with professional guidance
### Foods and drinks to avoid during IVF
To give your body the best support during IVF, try to limit or avoid:

- Caffeine
- Alcohol
- Highly processed foods
- Trans fats (often found in fried or packaged foods)
