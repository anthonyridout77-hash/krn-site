## Guiding you through the hormonal ups and downs of pregnancy

There are many factors that can influence your ability to conceive and nurture a healthy pregnancy. Your body is most fertile when your hormones are in balance and you are caring for your overall health. Building supportive daily habits around nutrition, movement, sleep and self-care can make a big difference in helping your body feel prepared for pregnancy.

I am here to help you find and maintain the right balance throughout your pregnancy journey. Together, we will prepare your mind and body while I guide and support you with up-to-date nutrition and health advice to help you adapt to the many changes pregnancy brings. Even small adjustments to your diet and lifestyle can make a big difference in how well you feel and how smoothly you navigate the ups and downs of pregnancy.

The hormonal changes that come with pregnancy can be surprising.

### Pregnant women experience many hormonal changes throughout their pregnancy journey that:

- Affect their mood
- Aid in fetal development
- Alter how the body reacts to physical activity
- Create the “pregnancy glow”The two main hormones that increase during pregnancy are estrogen and progesterone.
Estrogen levels increase during pregnancy to help the uterus and placenta:

- Transfer nutrients
- Support the baby’s development and maturing
- Improve the formation of blood vesselsProgesterone levels increase during pregnancy to:

- Aid the laxity of ligaments and joints throughout the body
- Aid internal structures (such as the uterus) to expand in size
- Aid internal structures to accommodate and develop along with a baby
### What happens when my hormones are out of balance?
Hormonal imbalances make it more difficult to get pregnant. If you are already pregnant and your hormones are out of balance, it can:

- Dysregulate and cause sudden changes in body temperature
- Cause indigestion and bloating
- Cause skin blemishes
- Cause mood swings and stress levels to fluctuate
- Cause irritability and trouble concentrating
### How nutrition supports you through the hormonal changes of pregnancy
Food cannot switch pregnancy hormones on or off, but what and how you eat can make many of their side effects easier to live with. Below are the changes I see most often, and the practical nutrition strategies we use for each.

### Nausea and food aversions (mostly weeks 6 to 14)
Rising hCG and oestrogen are linked with morning sickness, which can happen at any time of day. Strategies that often help:

- Small, frequent meals and snacks so your stomach is never completely empty
- Plain, dry or cold foods such as crackers, toast, rice cakes, fruit and yoghurt
- Ginger in tea, biscuits or lollies
- Sipping fluids between meals rather than with them
- Taking your prenatal supplement with food or at night if it makes you queasyIf you cannot keep fluids down, are losing weight or feel dizzy, please see your GP or midwife promptly, as this may be hyperemesis gravidarum and needs medical care.

### Heartburn, bloating and constipation
Progesterone relaxes smooth muscle throughout the body, which slows digestion and loosens the valve at the top of the stomach. Eating smaller meals, staying upright for an hour after eating, limiting very rich or spicy meals at night, and building up fibre slowly alongside plenty of water can all help.

### Blood glucose, hunger and cravings
From the second trimester, placental hormones make the body more resistant to insulin so more glucose reaches your baby. For some women this leads to gestational diabetes, which is screened for at 24 to 28 weeks. Pairing carbohydrate foods with protein, choosing wholegrains and spreading carbohydrate across the day help keep energy steadier. Read more in Gestational diabetes: just diagnosed, what now? (https://www.katridout.com.au/blogs/gestational-diabetes-just-diagnosed-what-now/)

### Thyroid hormones and iodine
Your thyroid works harder in pregnancy, and your baby relies on your thyroid hormone for brain development. Australian guidelines recommend a daily 150 microgram iodine supplement for women who are pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive. If you have a thyroid condition, check with your doctor before starting iodine.

### Mood, sleep and energy
Tiredness and mood changes are common as hormones shift. Low iron, vitamin B12 or vitamin D can add to fatigue, so it is worth checking your blood results. Regular meals, enough protein, omega-3 rich foods such as oily fish (two to three serves a week of low-mercury fish) and good hydration all support steadier energy. See Iron in pregnancy (https://www.katridout.com.au/blogs/iron-in-pregnancy-tired-before-anaemia/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

Hormones play a crucial role in pregnancy by regulating various physiological changes that support fetal development. Key hormones like human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), progesterone, and oestrogen are essential in maintaining a healthy pregnancy. They aid in placenta development, promote the baby’s growth, and prepare the body for childbirth. These hormones also influence metabolism and appetite, which is why guidance from a pregnancy dietitian and nutritionist can be beneficial in ensuring proper nutrition. A well-balanced diet, including healthy pregnancy recipes, supports both maternal and fetal health. For personalised advice on suitable diet plans or nutrition in Brisbane, consulting Katrina Ridout can help create a tailored approach to meet your specific needs.

During pregnancy, maintaining balanced hormones is crucial for both the mother and the developing baby. Foods rich in healthy fats, such as avocados, nuts, and seeds, can support hormone production. Additionally, incorporating lean proteins like chicken, fish, and legumes helps stabilise blood sugar levels, which in turn balances hormones. Katrina Ridout, an Accredited Practising Dietitian & Nutritionist, emphasises the importance of nutrient-dense, whole foods to support hormonal balance throughout pregnancy. Hydration and regular meals with a mix of complex carbs, protein, and healthy fats are key for optimal hormone regulation.

Hormone levels shift dramatically during pregnancy to support your baby’s development and prepare your body for birth. In the early weeks, hormones like human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) rise quickly to support the pregnancy. Progesterone increases to maintain the uterine lining, while oestrogen helps your baby grow and gets your breasts ready for milk production. As pregnancy progresses, relaxin rises to prepare your body for labour and delivery.

These changes are natural, but they can also affect how you feel physically and emotionally. The right nutrition can play a big role in helping you adapt to these changes. As a pregnancy dietitian, I provide personalised guidance to help you maintain a balanced diet that supports your hormones, keeps you nourished and makes you feel more confident throughout your pregnancy journey.

These hormones also influence your metabolism, mood and immune system throughout pregnancy.
