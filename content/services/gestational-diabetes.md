## What is gestational diabetes?

Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is a type of diabetes that develops during pregnancy. The placenta produces hormones that interfere with the action of insulin, the hormone that regulates blood sugar. When the body cannot produce enough insulin to meet the increased demand, blood sugar levels rise. GDM typically develops around 24 to 28 weeks, when insulin resistance increases due to hormonal changes.

It is different from type 1 diabetes (an autoimmune condition where the body does not produce insulin) and type 2 diabetes (where the body does not use insulin properly or does not make enough). GDM usually goes away after birth, but it does increase the risk of developing type 2 diabetes later in life.

## How gestational diabetes can affect you and your baby

If GDM is not well managed with diet, exercise and sometimes medication, it can cause complications for both you and your baby.

- For mums, this may include a higher chance of preeclampsia, the need for a caesarean section, and a greater risk of type 2 diabetes or cardiovascular disease later in life.
- For babies, this may include being larger than average (macrosomia), which can sometimes lead to an emergency caesarean or pre-term labour, as well as low blood sugar after birth, jaundice, breathing difficulties, and an increased risk of obesity or type 2 diabetes as they grow older.

The encouraging news is that with the right care, including healthy lifestyle changes, blood sugar monitoring and medication if needed, these risks can be significantly reduced.

## How is it diagnosed?

All women are screened for gestational diabetes between 24 and 28 weeks of pregnancy. This is usually done with blood tests and an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT), where you drink a sweet liquid and your blood sugar is measured over time. The likelihood of developing GDM varies with factors such as age, ethnicity and existing health conditions. Your doctor, midwife or dietitian can guide you through the process and explain your results.

## Quick tips for managing GDM

Some simple steps that can make a big difference:

- Eat regular meals and snacks to keep blood sugar levels stable
- Choose wholegrain and low-GI carbohydrates for slow, steady energy
- Pair carbohydrates with protein and healthy fats to balance blood sugar
- Stay active with gentle exercise like walking, if recommended by your doctor
- Monitor your blood sugar levels as advised by your healthcare team
- Stay hydrated with plenty of water throughout the day

## How is gestational diabetes managed?

GDM can often be managed well through diet and lifestyle changes. A dietitian can help you create a meal plan that regulates carbohydrate intake so blood sugar stays steadier, which typically means smaller, more frequent meals rich in fibre and nutrients, with sugary foods and drinks kept to a minimum. Regular physical activity, as recommended by your healthcare provider, also helps. Monitoring your blood sugar and taking any prescribed medication as directed are important parts of the plan, and in some cases insulin may be needed. Regular antenatal check-ups keep an eye on both you and your baby.
