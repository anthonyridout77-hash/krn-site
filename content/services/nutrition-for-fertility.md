## The right nutrition can help prepare your body for pregnancy

### Evidence-based nutritional support to improve your chances of successfully conceiving
If you’re in the pre-conception or planning stage and want to give yourself the best chance of falling pregnant, one of the most powerful tools you can use is evidence-based nutrition — personalised to support your fertility.
I work alongside leading obstetricians, gynaecologists, endocrinologists, GPs and other early-life nutrition experts to help women and couples improve their nutrition and boost their fertility outcomes.
Every day, I support mums- and dads-to-be with tailored nutrition plans designed to prepare the body for conception, pregnancy and beyond. Whether your goal is to improve egg or sperm quality, boost egg count, balance fertility hormones, or prepare your uterine lining and body for implantation, I can provide the guidance and support you need.

### Nutrition expertise backed by evidence-based research
The nutritional support I provide is grounded in the latest clinical research, so you can feel confident you’re receiving advice you can trust. Alongside personalised guidance, I offer fertility meal plans, recipes, education and practical strategies designed to boost your chances of conceiving and supporting a healthy pregnancy and baby.
If you are underweight or living with an underlying health condition that may impact fertility — such as PCOS, endometriosis, adenomyosis, or autoimmune conditions like Coeliac or Crohn’s disease — I can help you manage your symptoms and prepare your body, eggs and sperm for conception. This can make a significant difference whether you are trying to conceive naturally or with the help of IVF.

### The fertility solution that’s tailored for you
Every person and every couple is unique; shaped by different bodies, diets, medical histories, genetics, lifestyles and preferences. That’s why I provide personalised nutrition advice that is tailored specifically to you, to help improve fertility and give you the best possible chance of conceiving.
With over fifteen years of experience in both private practice and high-level research in fertility, pregnancy and early life nutrition, I specialise in helping women and couples optimise their nutrition to support conception and a healthy pregnancy. There are a number of fertility-boosting foods and nutrients that can make a real difference. Eating the right foods and taking the right supplements at the right time can significantly improve your chances of conceiving and carrying a healthy pregnancy. Because everyone’s journey is different, I’ll work with you to create a plan that’s tailored to your body and your needs.

Limiting caffeine to moderate levels while trying to conceive, and focusing instead on nutrient-rich foods, can help support fertility and healthy hormone levels.

A healthy, balanced diet can make a big difference to fertility for both men and women. Choosing mostly whole and minimally processed foods, plenty of plant-based options, healthy fats, lean proteins, iron-rich foods and low-glycaemic index carbohydrates can all support reproductive health.

It’s also important to limit or avoid things that may reduce fertility, such as trans fats, excess caffeine, alcohol and smoking.

Rather than relying on single “fertility foods,” the goal is to eat a variety of nutrient-dense foods that work together to support your reproductive health. Folate (or folic acid) is one of the most important nutrients for fertility. It supports DNA synthesis and cell division, which are vital for the development of healthy eggs and sperm. Adequate folate levels also help regulate hormones, support regular ovulation, contribute to sperm health and may reduce the risk of miscarriage. Once pregnancy occurs, folate plays a key role in preventing neural tube defects in early development.

Folate is found naturally in foods such as leafy greens, legumes and fortified cereals, but supplementation is usually recommended for women trying to conceive to ensure optimal levels. Working with a healthcare provider, such as Kat Ridout, can help you determine the right dosage to best support your reproductive health and fertility journey.

Carrying excess weight can have an impact on female fertility. It may lead to hormonal imbalances, affect egg quality, reduce the body’s response to fertility treatments, and increase the risk of pregnancy complications. These factors can make it harder to conceive, but there is good news — even modest weight loss through healthy eating and regular movement can improve fertility outcomes.

Working with a healthcare provider or a registered dietitian can help you create a safe, personalised plan to support healthy weight management, balance hormones and boost your chances of conceiving.

Key nutrients include folate, zinc, iron, omega-3 fatty acids, vitamin D, and antioxidants. These support hormone regulation, egg quality, and sperm function. For example, folate reduces the risk of early pregnancy complications, while zinc helps sperm production. Katrina Ridout highlights the importance of meeting these needs through balanced meals or supplements where necessary, helping both women and men prepare their bodies for conception.

Yes, gut health can play an important role in fertility. A healthy gut microbiome helps regulate hormones, support nutrient absorption and reduce inflammation — all of which are important for reproductive health. Poor gut health, on the other hand, can contribute to imbalances that affect ovulation, egg and sperm quality, and even the ability of an embryo to implant.

Looking after your gut through a balanced diet rich in fibre, probiotics, prebiotics and whole foods can help support both fertility and overall wellbeing. If you’re trying to conceive, working with a dietitian can give you personalised strategies to optimise your gut health and boost your fertility naturally.

Yes, certain foods should be avoided or limited when trying to conceive to support optimal fertility and reproductive health:

Saturated Fats: Found in many processed and fried foods, saturated fats can disrupt hormone levels and negatively impact fertility. Opt for healthier fats, such as those found in avocados, nuts, and olive oil.

Excessive Sugar: High sugar intake can lead to insulin resistance and hormonal imbalances, affecting ovulation and sperm health. Limit sugary snacks and beverages, and focus on whole, unprocessed foods.

High-Mercury Fish: Fish such as sharks, swordfish, and king mackerel contain high levels of mercury, which can affect foetal development and overall fertility. Opt for lower-mercury options like salmon, sardines, and trout.

Excessive Caffeine: High caffeine consumption can negatively impact fertility by affecting hormone levels and ovulation. Limit intake to under 200mg per day.

Alcohol: Excessive alcohol consumption can interfere with hormone balance, ovulation, and sperm quality. It is advisable to reduce or eliminate alcohol intake when trying to conceive.

Processed Meats: Linked to reduced sperm quality and potential negative effects on overall fertility, it’s best to limit processed meats such as bacon, sausages, and deli meats.

Artificial Sweeteners: Some studies suggest that artificial sweeteners may affect hormone levels and fertility, so consider reducing their use and opting for natural sweeteners in moderation.

Focusing on a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean proteins, and healthy fats can help support fertility and overall health.
