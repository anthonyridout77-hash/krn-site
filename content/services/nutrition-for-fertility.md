## Frequently asked questions

### Which foods support fertility?

A healthy, balanced diet can make a big difference for both men and women. Choosing mostly whole and minimally processed foods, plenty of plant foods, healthy fats, lean proteins, iron-rich foods and low-glycaemic index carbohydrates supports hormone balance and healthy egg and sperm production. Rather than relying on single "fertility foods", the goal is a variety of nutrient-dense foods that work together.

### Which nutrients matter most?

Key nutrients include folate, zinc, iron, omega-3 fats, vitamin D and antioxidants.

- Folate supports DNA synthesis and cell division for healthy eggs and sperm, helps regulate hormones and ovulation, and once pregnancy occurs helps prevent neural tube defects. It is found in leafy greens, legumes and fortified cereals, and supplementation is usually recommended for women trying to conceive.
- Iron is essential for ovulation and carrying oxygen to reproductive organs. Low iron can disrupt cycles. Lean meats, legumes and leafy greens are good sources.
- Vitamin D influences hormone regulation and egg quality. Low levels may be linked to reduced fertility, so it is worth checking through diet, safe sun exposure or supplements.
- Zinc helps sperm production, and omega-3 fats and antioxidants support hormone regulation and egg quality.

### What should a balanced fertility meal look like?

Lean protein, high-fibre whole grains, colourful vegetables, fruit, and healthy fats like avocado or nuts. Spacing meals regularly helps keep blood sugar and hormones steady.

### Does caffeine affect fertility?

High caffeine intake may affect hormone balance and reduce the chances of conception. Moderate consumption is generally considered safe; the usual guide is to keep intake under 200 mg a day while trying to conceive.

### Are there foods to avoid when trying to conceive?

It helps to limit or avoid:

- Trans and saturated fats found in many processed and fried foods
- Excess sugar, which can contribute to insulin resistance and hormone imbalance
- High-mercury fish such as shark, swordfish and king mackerel; choose lower-mercury options like salmon, sardines and trout
- Excess caffeine and alcohol, which can interfere with hormone balance, ovulation and sperm quality
- Processed meats such as bacon, sausages and deli meats, linked to reduced sperm quality
- Artificial sweeteners, which some studies suggest may affect hormone levels
- Smoking

### Does weight affect fertility?

Carrying excess weight may lead to hormonal imbalances, affect egg quality, reduce the body's response to fertility treatments and increase the risk of pregnancy complications. Even modest weight change through healthy eating and regular movement can help. A dietitian can help you build a safe, personalised plan.

### Does gut health play a role?

Yes. A healthy gut microbiome helps regulate hormones, support nutrient absorption and reduce inflammation, all of which matter for reproductive health. A diet rich in fibre, probiotics, prebiotics and whole foods supports both gut health and fertility.
