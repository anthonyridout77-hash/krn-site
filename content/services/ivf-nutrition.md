If you are struggling to conceive, you are not alone. Around one in six couples need extra support on their journey to pregnancy, and for many, IVF or another assisted treatment is part of that path. Alongside the medical care of your fertility clinic, the right nutrition can make a meaningful difference to how prepared and supported you feel. ## What IVF involves

IVF (in vitro fertilisation) is one of the options that can help make having a biological child possible for women who have experienced fertility or medical challenges. Other treatments include ovulation induction, assisted insemination (IUI) and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).

During IVF, an egg is retrieved and fertilised with sperm in a laboratory to create an embryo, which is then transferred into the uterus in the hope that it implants and grows into a healthy pregnancy. Medical specialists monitor progress closely with blood tests and ultrasounds.

## The lead-up to treatment

In the month before your IVF cycle, nourishing your body with a healthy, balanced and personalised diet can help you feel as strong and prepared as possible. It is a chance to gently set the foundations for the process ahead.

It is also normal to feel many different emotions at this stage. Sadness, anxiety or uncertainty are common before, during and after IVF. The journey can be physically and emotionally demanding, and you do not have to go through it alone.

## What to eat during IVF

Good nutrition matters for both partners in the lead-up to and during a cycle, because diet supports both egg and sperm health. Rather than sudden or extreme changes, the focus is on nourishing, sustainable choices. For many couples, a Mediterranean-style diet rich in whole foods, healthy fats and plant foods provides a strong foundation. Exact nutrient needs vary from person to person, which is why personalised advice is so valuable.

If you are trying to conceive, it helps to:

- Include healthy fats such as avocado, nuts, seeds and olive oil
- Eat a variety of leafy greens and colourful vegetables
- Include a source of protein with every meal
- Choose whole grains for fibre and steady energy
- Include dairy or fortified alternatives for calcium and vitamin D
- Stay well hydrated with plenty of water
- Limit caffeine and avoid alcohol where possible
- Take a daily prenatal multivitamin supplement
- Manage any existing dietary or medical conditions with professional guidance

### Foods and drinks to limit or avoid

To give your body the most support during IVF, try to limit or avoid:

- Caffeine and alcohol
- Highly processed foods with added sugars or unhealthy fats
- Trans fats, often found in fried or packaged foods
- Fish high in mercury, such as swordfish or king mackerel
- Unpasteurised dairy products and unwashed fruit and vegetables
- Soy products high in phytoestrogens
- Excessive red meat and high-sodium foods
- Artificial sweeteners

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I take folic acid during IVF?

Yes. Folic acid is an essential nutrient during IVF and early pregnancy. It supports healthy cell division and the development of the neural tube, reducing the risk of birth defects such as spina bifida. Kat recommends that women begin taking folic acid before and during IVF treatment. Eating folate-rich foods such as leafy greens, legumes and fortified grains adds further support. For personalised advice on supplements, speak with your dietitian or fertility specialist.

### Are healthy fats important?

Yes. Healthy fats support hormone production and egg quality and play a role in overall reproductive health. Good sources include avocados, olive oil, nuts, seeds and oily fish. Omega-3 fats, found in salmon, chia seeds and flaxseeds, help reduce inflammation and support the reproductive system.

### Which foods support fertility?

Leafy greens for folate, whole grains for steady blood sugar, lean proteins such as beans, lentils, eggs and chicken, and antioxidant-rich fruits such as berries and citrus, which help protect eggs and sperm from oxidative stress. They work alongside hydration, stress management and gentle regular exercise.
