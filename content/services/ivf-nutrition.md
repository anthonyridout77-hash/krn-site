## Feel confident and supported as you prepare for IVF and fertility treatments with Kat Ridout by your side.

If you are struggling to conceive, you are not alone. Around one in six couples need extra support on their journey to pregnancy.
For women who are ready to start a family or add another child, and who have experienced fertility or medical challenges, IVF (in vitro fertilisation) is often one of the options that can help make having a biological child possible. Other treatments may include ovulation induction, assisted insemination (IUI) or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).
During IVF, an egg is retrieved and fertilised with sperm in a laboratory to create an embryo. This embryo is then transferred into the uterus, where the hope is that it will implant and grow into a healthy pregnancy. The process is closely supported by medical specialists, who monitor progress with blood tests and ultrasounds.
Alongside this medical care, the right nutrition can make a meaningful difference. Your clinic manages the medical side. Kat focuses on the three months of nutrition around your cycle and helps you trim your supplement list to what the evidence supports. Kat combines evidence-based nutrition with compassionate support to help you feel confident and cared for throughout your journey.

### The lead up to IVF treatment
In the month before beginning your IVF cycle, nourishing your body with a healthy, balanced and personalised diet can help you feel as strong and prepared as possible. This time is an opportunity to gently set the foundations for the process ahead.
It is also normal to experience many different emotions during this stage. Feelings of sadness, anxiety or uncertainty can be common before, during and after IVF. The journey can be physically and emotionally demanding, and it is important to remember you do not have to go through it alone. With the right guidance and support, this time can feel more manageable and positive.

### What to eat during IVF
In the lead up to and during an IVF cycle, good nutrition plays a vital role for both men and women. Supporting sperm health and egg health through diet can make a real difference in giving the process the best chance of success.
Rather than making sudden or extreme changes, it is best to focus on nourishing, sustainable choices. For many couples, following a Mediterranean-style diet rich in whole foods, healthy fats and plant-based nutrients provides an excellent foundation. The exact nutrients needed will vary from person to person, which is why personalised advice is so valuable.
Kat Ridout works with individuals and couples to create realistic, evidence-based nutrition plans that support both fertility and wellbeing throughout the IVF journey. Her goal is to help you feel confident, cared for and empowered every step of the way.
For those looking to conceive, it is important to:

- Include healthy fats such as avocado, nuts, seeds and olive oil
- Eat a variety of leafy greens and colourful vegetables
- Include a source of protein with every meal
- Stay well hydrated by drinking plenty of water
- Limit caffeine and avoid alcohol where possible
- Take a daily prenatal multivitamin supplement
- Manage any existing dietary or medical conditions with professional guidance

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is folic acid important during IVF?

Yes. Folic acid is an essential nutrient during IVF and early pregnancy. It supports healthy cell division and helps with the development of the neural tube, reducing the risk of birth defects such as spina bifida.

Katrina Ridout, an Accredited Practising Dietitian specialising in fertility and pregnancy, recommends that women begin taking folic acid supplements before and during IVF treatment to prepare the body for a healthy pregnancy.

In addition to supplements, eating foods rich in folate — such as leafy greens, legumes and fortified grains — can further support fertility and early fetal development.

For personalised advice on supplementation and nutrition during IVF, it’s best to work with a dietitian or speak with your fertility specialist.

### How important is diet during IVF?

Diet plays an important role in supporting IVF success. A well-balanced and nutritious diet can:

- Help you achieve and maintain a healthy weight

- Provide essential nutrients for egg and embryo development

- Regulate blood sugar levels and reduce inflammation

- Support hormone production and balance

- Optimise overall reproductive health

Because every woman’s body and fertility journey is unique, personalised advice can make a real difference. Working with a fertility dietitian like Katrina Ridout ensures that your nutrition plan is tailored to your needs, helping you feel confident and supported throughout the IVF process.

### What foods should be avoided during IVF?

During IVF, it is best to limit or avoid foods and drinks that may interfere with hormone balance or increase inflammation. These include:

- Highly processed foods with added sugars or unhealthy fats

- Excess caffeine and alcohol

- Soy products high in phytoestrogens

- Fish high in mercury (such as swordfish or king mackerel)

- Unpasteurised dairy products

- Unwashed fruits and vegetables

- Excessive red meat and high-sodium foods

- Artificial sweeteners

Every woman’s nutritional needs are different, so personalised advice is important. Working with a fertility dietitian like Katrina Ridout can help you create a safe, balanced and supportive nutrition plan for your IVF journey.

### Should I include healthy fats in my IVF diet?

Yes. Healthy fats are an important part of an IVF-friendly diet. They are part of a balanced eating pattern before and during treatment.

Good sources of healthy fats include avocados, olive oil, nuts, seeds and oily fish. Omega-3 fatty acids, found in foods like salmon, chia seeds and flaxseeds, are especially beneficial as they help reduce inflammation and support the reproductive system.

Katrina Ridout, an Accredited Practising Dietitian specialising in fertility and pregnancy, encourages women to include these nourishing fats as part of a balanced diet to give their bodies the best foundation during IVF.

### What foods best complement an IVF treatment?

A balanced, whole-food diet can support your body through IVF and improve reproductive health. Focus on:

- Plenty of fruits and vegetables for vitamins, minerals and antioxidants

- Whole grains for fibre and steady energy

- Lean proteins such as chicken, fish, legumes and eggs

- Healthy fats from avocados, nuts, seeds and olive oil

- Dairy or fortified alternatives to boost calcium and vitamin D

- Adequate hydration with plenty of water

It’s also important to be mindful with caffeine, practise stress management and maintain gentle, regular exercise.

For tailored support, working with a fertility dietitian like Katrina Ridout can ensure your nutrition plan is designed to meet your specific needs, alongside your clinic's medical plan.

### Are there specific foods to focus on during IVF?

Yes. Certain foods can help support reproductive health during IVF by providing key nutrients as part of an overall balanced pattern; no single food changes IVF results. Katrina Ridout, an Accredited Practising Dietitian specialising in fertility and pregnancy, emphasises that these foods are most effective when part of a balanced lifestyle that also includes hydration, stress management and healthy daily habits.

Some fertility-supportive foods include:

- Leafy greens and vegetables: rich in folate, a vital nutrient for fertility

- Whole grains: fibre and steadier energy

- Healthy fats: omega-3s from salmon, walnuts and flaxseeds support hormone regulation

- Lean proteins: beans, lentils, eggs and chicken provide building blocks for reproductive health

- Antioxidant-rich fruits: berries and citrus help protect eggs and sperm from oxidative stress

Kat often recommends weaving these ingredients into simple, nourishing meals that fit your lifestyle. For personalised advice, it is best to consult with a fertility dietitian who can tailor recommendations to your unique needs.
