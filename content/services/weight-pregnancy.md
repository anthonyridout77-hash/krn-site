Reaching a healthy weight can feel challenging at any time, and even more so when emotions and hormones are changing in the lead-up to and during pregnancy. The good news is that these same changes are what help your body conceive and carry a baby. Supporting your body with balanced nutrition and healthy habits sets you up for a smoother, healthier pregnancy, and it is never too late to begin. This page is part of Kat's [pregnancy nutrition](/our-services/pregnancy-nutrition-and-dietetics/) support.

## Your BMI and pregnancy weight gain

Body mass index (BMI) is a tool used to guide how much weight gain is recommended during pregnancy. The categories are:

- Below 18.5: underweight
- 18.5 to 24.9: healthy weight range
- 25.0 to 29.9: overweight
- 30.0 and above: obese

For the recommended weight gain range for each category, see the [Pregnancy Weight](/our-services/pregnancy-nutrition-and-dietetics/pregnancy-weight/) page.

## What can influence how much weight you gain

- The types of foods you eat and how you eat them
- Your activity levels during pregnancy
- Pregnancy symptoms such as nausea and fatigue
- Changes in appetite, including food cravings and aversions
- Fluid retention

## Key nutrients during pregnancy

The major nutrients to include in your diet are:

- Protein (cooked meat, chicken, fish, beans, dairy, soy products)
- Folate (legumes, leafy greens, asparagus, eggs, beetroot, citrus)
- Iron (poultry, fish, eggs, beans, leafy greens, nuts, seeds, wholegrains)
- Iodine (grains, dairy, cooked fish, seaweed, fruit and vegetables)
- Vitamin D (oily fish, fortified soy milk or orange juice, cheese, eggs)

## Food safety during pregnancy

During pregnancy your immune system naturally lowers to help protect your growing baby. This makes you more vulnerable to foodborne illnesses such as listeria and salmonella, which is why food safety is especially important. It is safest to avoid foods with a higher risk of contamination: raw or undercooked meat and eggs, soft cheeses, chilled seafood, deli meats, unpasteurised milk or juice, and pre-prepared salads. Kat can guide you with safe alternatives so you never feel restricted. Read more in [What foods should you avoid during pregnancy in Australia?](/blogs/foods-to-avoid-during-pregnancy-australia/)

While your immune system changes in pregnancy, good nutrition helps keep it strong. A balanced diet rich in vitamins, minerals, antioxidants, lean proteins and healthy fats supports both your immunity and your baby's growth.
