## Frequently asked questions

### Why do nutrient deficiencies matter in children?

If children do not get enough of certain nutrients, deficiencies can affect their health and development. Too little iron can cause anaemia, low calcium can lead to weak bones, not enough zinc may slow growth, and a lack of iodine can affect thyroid function. Iron deficiency is the most common nutritional deficiency in children, although its prevalence in the first year of life has been decreasing with improved iron supplementation. A balanced diet with a wide variety of nutrient-rich foods supports immunity, growth and cognitive development.

### What are the signs of a deficiency?

Signs vary depending on the nutrient. Iron deficiency may cause pale skin, tiredness and weakness. Low vitamin D can lead to rickets, and vitamin A deficiency may cause night blindness. Calcium deficiency can result in weak bones, and low zinc may slow growth and weaken the immune system. Not all children with deficiencies show obvious symptoms, which is why a balanced diet and regular check-ups with your healthcare provider matter.

### Which first foods are gentle on digestion?

There is no single recommended first food. Current guidance recommends introducing a variety of foods, including common allergens, early to help reduce the risk of food allergy. If using rice-based products, choose low-arsenic options and vary grains where possible. Foods that are generally gentle on a baby's digestion include:

- Banana, avocado and pear, which are soft and easily mashed
- Sweet potato, carrot and peas, cooked and mashed or pureed
- Cooked apple puree, starting smooth and moving to small chunks as your baby grows
- Plain full-fat yoghurt from around 6 months, with no added sugar
- Oats as a porridge or mixed with pureed fruit

Introduce new foods one at a time and watch for signs of allergy or digestive discomfort. Talk to your dietitian or GP if you have concerns.

### What do poor feeding habits look like?

Under- or overeating, not enough nutritious foods, or too many foods and drinks high in fat, salt or sugar and low in fibre. Examples include overfeeding, bottle propping, introducing solids too early, ignoring hunger cues, offering inappropriate foods, too much snacking, or using food as a reward or punishment. Establishing healthy feeding practices from the beginning supports your baby's growth and wellbeing.

### Which foods support brain development?

Breast milk provides essential fatty acids, antibodies and nutrients that support brain development, and infant formula is formulated to provide the nutrients needed for growth when breastfeeding is not possible. As solids are introduced, foods that support brain development include oily fish such as salmon and sardines for omega-3 DHA, eggs for choline and protein, leafy greens, berries, whole grains, Greek yoghurt, nuts and seeds (in safe forms), and avocado. Introduce solids gradually and take any specific dietary needs or allergies into account.
