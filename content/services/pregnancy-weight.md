Pregnancy brings big changes to your body, including natural weight gain as your baby grows. Many mums wonder whether they are gaining the right amount or getting the right nutrients. The weight you gain is not just baby or fat. It includes blood volume, fluid and the placenta, all essential to your baby's growth. This page is part of Kat's [pregnancy nutrition](/our-services/pregnancy-nutrition-and-dietetics/) support.

## What makes up weight gain in pregnancy?

Weight gain is a combination of many changes your body makes to support a healthy pregnancy:

- Your growing baby
- The enlarging uterus
- The placenta
- Breast enlargement
- Extra fat stores for energy and breastfeeding
- Increased fluid and blood volume

All of these are natural and necessary for your baby's development and your own health.

## Recommended weight gain ranges in Australia

Healthy weight gain looks different for every woman and depends on your starting weight, body type and individual health needs. Australian pregnancy care uses the Institute of Medicine ranges, based on your pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI). For a single baby, the total recommended gain is:

- BMI under 18.5: 12.5 to 18 kg
- BMI 18.5 to 24.9: 11.5 to 16 kg
- BMI 25 to 29.9: 7 to 11.5 kg
- BMI 30 or over: 5 to 9 kg

Ranges are different for twins. They are a guide, not a target to chase week by week. Most gain happens in the second and third trimesters, and your GP, midwife or obstetrician will keep an eye on how you are tracking.

## How much extra do you actually need to eat?

"Eating for two" is one of the most common pregnancy myths. According to Australian nutrient reference values, most women need no extra energy in the first trimester, around 1,400 kJ extra a day in the second trimester and around 1,900 kJ extra a day in the third. That is roughly one or two extra nourishing snacks a day, such as a wholegrain sandwich with egg or tuna, or yoghurt with fruit and a handful of nuts.

## Nutrients matter more than numbers

Your needs for several nutrients rise far more than your energy needs do, so the quality of what you eat matters most. Key nutrients in pregnancy include:

- Folate: a daily folic acid supplement of at least 400 micrograms, ideally from before conception to 12 weeks
- Iodine: a daily 150 microgram supplement for your baby's brain development
- Iron: from lean red meat, legumes and fortified cereals, with levels checked through pregnancy
- Calcium and vitamin D: from dairy or calcium-fortified alternatives, and safe sun exposure
- Omega-3 fats: from two to three serves a week of low-mercury fish
- Protein: at every meal to support your baby's growth. See [protein in pregnancy](/blogs/protein-in-pregnancy/)

## Why it is worth getting support early

Gaining well above or below the recommended range is linked with a higher chance of complications such as gestational diabetes and differences in your baby's birth weight. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I lose weight during pregnancy?

Pregnancy is not the time to focus on weight loss. The goal is to support your baby's growth and your own wellbeing with balanced, nourishing foods. With the right guidance, you can manage your weight in a healthy way that benefits you both.

### I have already gained more than recommended. Is it too late?

Please do not be hard on yourself. Every pregnancy is different and weight gain varies widely. It is never too late to make small, positive changes to your nutrition and lifestyle, and even gentle adjustments can help you feel better.

### What are good snacks for a healthy pregnancy weight?

Fresh fruit, nuts, Greek yoghurt, wholegrain crackers with avocado, and vegetable sticks with hummus. These give you essential nutrients without excess energy and help manage the appetite changes that come with pregnancy hormones.

### How do I manage cravings?

Drink plenty of water and eat regular meals to reduce the sudden hunger that often triggers cravings. Plan balanced snacks with protein, fibre and healthy fats, choose nutritious alternatives where you can, and keep portions moderate. Cravings are a normal part of pregnancy.
