## What does healthy weight gain look like during pregnancy?

It’s no secret that pregnancy brings significant changes to your body, including natural weight gain as your baby grows. Your body is working hard to nurture and care for your little one, and part of that process is preparing itself for a healthy pregnancy and birth.
As your body adjusts, your diet, cravings and nutritional needs will shift too. This can leave many mums wondering: Am I getting the right nutrients for my baby? Am I gaining the right amount of weight?
Healthy weight gain in pregnancy depends on your starting weight and body type, and it’s important to remember that the weight you gain is not just baby or fat. It also includes things like increased blood volume, amniotic fluid and the placenta — all of which are essential to your baby’s growth and development.

### What makes up weight gain during pregnancy?
Weight gain in pregnancy isn’t just about your baby — it’s a combination of many important changes your body makes to support a healthy pregnancy, including:

- Your growing baby
- The enlarging uterus
- The placenta
- Breast enlargement
- Extra fat stores for energy and breastfeeding
- Increased fluid and blood volumeAll of these changes are completely natural and essential for your baby’s growth and development, as well as your own health during pregnancy.

### What is a healthy weight gain during pregnancy?
Healthy weight gain during pregnancy looks different for every woman. It depends on your starting weight, body type and individual health needs. The important thing is making sure that both you and your baby are well nourished and supported throughout your pregnancy.

### Recommended weight gain ranges in Australia
Australian pregnancy care uses the Institute of Medicine ranges, based on your pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI). For a single baby, the total recommended gain is:

- BMI under 18.5: 12.5 to 18 kg
- BMI 18.5 to 24.9: 11.5 to 16 kg
- BMI 25 to 29.9: 7 to 11.5 kg
- BMI 30 or over: 5 to 9 kgRanges are different for twins. They are a guide, not a target to chase week by week. Most gain happens in the second and third trimesters, and your GP, midwife or obstetrician will keep an eye on how you are tracking.

### How much extra do you actually need to eat?
The idea of eating for two is one of the most common pregnancy myths. According to Australian nutrient reference values, most women need no extra energy in the first trimester, around 1,400 kJ extra a day in the second trimester and around 1,900 kJ extra a day in the third. That is roughly one or two extra nourishing snacks a day, such as a wholegrain sandwich with egg or tuna, or yoghurt with fruit and a handful of nuts.

### Nutrients matter more than numbers
Your needs for several nutrients rise far more than your energy needs do, so the quality of what you eat matters most. Key nutrients in pregnancy include:

- Folate: a daily folic acid supplement of at least 400 micrograms, ideally from before conception to 12 weeks
- Iodine: a daily 150 microgram supplement for brain development
- Iron: from lean red meat, legumes and fortified cereals, with levels checked through pregnancy
- Calcium and vitamin D: from dairy or calcium-fortified alternatives, and safe sun exposure
- Omega-3 fats: from two to three serves a week of low-mercury fish
- Protein: at every meal to support your baby’s growth. See protein in pregnancy
### Why it is worth getting support early
Gaining well above or below the recommended range is linked with a higher chance of complications such as gestational diabetes and differences in your baby’s birth weight. That is not a reason to diet in pregnancy. It is a reason to get practical, non-judgemental support that fits your appetite, culture, budget and energy levels. ## Frequently Asked Questions

Pregnancy is not the time to focus on weight loss. Instead, the goal is to support your baby’s growth and your own wellbeing with balanced, nourishing foods. With the right guidance, you can manage your weight in a healthy way that benefits both you and your baby.

Pregnancy weight gain is influenced by several factors, including the baby’s growth, increased blood volume, and fluid retention. Nutritional needs also change to support both mother and baby. Genetics, pre-pregnancy weight, and activity levels also play important roles. Additionally, hormonal changes can affect metabolism and appetite. It’s important to focus on balanced nutrition and regular check-ups with healthcare providers to ensure healthy weight gain during pregnancy.

Healthy weight gain is different for every woman and depends on your pre-pregnancy weight, body type and overall health. What matters most is that you and your baby are well nourished. I’ll guide you in understanding what’s right for your body and help you feel confident in your pregnancy journey.

Please don’t be hard on yourself; every pregnancy is different, and weight gain can vary widely. It’s never too late to make small, positive changes to your nutrition and lifestyle. Even gentle adjustments can help you feel better and support a healthy pregnancy for you and your baby.

Nutritious snacks that support a healthy pregnancy weight include fresh fruits, nuts, Greek yoghurt, wholegrain crackers with avocado, and vegetable sticks with hummus. Katrina Ridout, an Accredited Practising Dietitian & Nutritionist based in Brisbane, recommends these choices as part of balanced pregnancy dishes. These snacks provide essential nutrients without excess calories, helping manage the hormonal changes during pregnancy that affect appetite. It’s also important to be mindful of things you can’t eat whilst pregnant, such as unpasteurised cheeses or raw seafood. For more nutritious recipes for pregnancy and personalised advice, consulting a trusted dietitian in Brisbane like Katrina Ridout can be very helpful.

Katrina Ridout recommends managing cravings by choosing nutritious alternatives and practising mindful eating. Drinking plenty of water and eating regular meals can help reduce sudden hunger that often triggers cravings. She advises planning balanced snacks that include protein, fibre, and healthy fats to maintain energy and satisfaction. Understanding cravings as a normal part of pregnancy helps, but keeping portion sizes moderate supports healthy weight management. Consulting a dietitian can provide personalised strategies tailored to your needs.
