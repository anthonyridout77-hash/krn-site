## Why postpartum nutrition matters

The weeks and months after birth are a time of healing, recovery and adjustment. Your body is recovering from labour while working hard to produce milk and support your baby's needs. Both childbirth and breastfeeding place high demands on your body, so your requirements for many key nutrients increase.

Nourishing yourself well helps your body heal, restores your energy and supports you in your new role. It also plays a part in mood and emotional wellbeing. Many women experience dips in mood after birth, and giving your body the nutrients it needs can help you feel stronger and more balanced.

## Quick tips for postnatal mums

- Stay hydrated, especially if breastfeeding
- Eat a wide variety of foods from the five core food groups
- Eat high-protein foods to rebuild tissues and support healing, as well as healthy hair, skin and nails
- Set up a routine for your meals and movement to get back on track
- Get as much sleep and rest as you can. When baby sleeps, you rest too (the laundry can wait)
- Keep pre-prepared, high-protein, high-carbohydrate snacks on hand for when you are tired or time-poor

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I eat after having a baby?

Focus on protein, iron, calcium, vitamin D and folate, with a variety of fruit, vegetables, whole grains and healthy fats, and plenty of water. Other vitamins that matter postnatally include vitamin C and the B vitamins, which support healing, immunity, bone health and energy metabolism. A balanced, varied diet is the main source, and if you are unsure whether you are getting enough, a dietitian can check and advise.

### Is there anything to avoid after birth?

To support healing and avoid discomfort, it helps to limit caffeine, alcohol, spicy or gas-producing foods, processed or high-fat foods, and raw or undercooked meat.

### How does my diet change when breastfeeding?

You will need more energy (around 300 to 500 extra calories a day), nutrient-rich foods, good hydration, continued prenatal vitamins and omega-3 fats, while limiting caffeine and alcohol and watching for any food sensitivities in your baby. For more, read [What should I eat while breastfeeding?](/blogs/what-should-i-eat-while-breastfeeding/)

### What are some quick, healthy snacks for breastfeeding?

- Fresh whole fruit such as apples, oranges, bananas or grapes
- Plain Greek yoghurt with a drizzle of honey or a handful of berries
- A handful of almonds, walnuts or pumpkin seeds
- Baby carrots or vegetable sticks with hummus or tzatziki
- Rice cakes or wholegrain crackers with natural nut butter
- Hard-boiled eggs prepared in advance
- A smoothie of fruit, leafy greens, Greek yoghurt and milk or a plant-based milk

Have a glass of water or herbal tea alongside your snack.

### Which foods help with blood pressure?

Leafy greens and bananas for potassium, berries for antioxidants and flavonoids, oats for fibre, Greek yoghurt for calcium and protein, garlic, dark chocolate (70 per cent or more, in moderation) and oily fish such as salmon, mackerel and sardines for omega-3 fats have all been associated with healthier blood pressure. They work as part of an overall balanced diet. If you have high blood pressure, speak with your healthcare professional or dietitian.
