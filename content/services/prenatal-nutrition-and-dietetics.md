Your health and wellbeing in the months leading up to pregnancy can make a meaningful difference to your ability to conceive. For some women, falling pregnant happens easily. For others it is a longer journey with ups and downs, and sometimes the challenges are completely outside your control. Individualised nutrition is one thing you can start today, and Kat is here to help you feel calm, supported and ready.

## Why pre-conception nutrition matters

A healthy weight, a balanced diet and a healthy lifestyle provide the foundation for conception, but every woman has unique nutritional needs shaped by her health, family history, lifestyle and fertility journey. Learning what your body needs during this time helps you prepare for conception and carry your baby with confidence.

Quick facts:

- The food you eat before pregnancy can influence your baby's long-term health through epigenetic programming: not by changing their DNA sequence, but by affecting how genes are expressed.
- Pregnancy is a window of opportunity to make lasting, positive changes to your lifestyle and wellbeing.
- It is never too late to improve your diet and lifestyle. Entering pregnancy at a healthy weight benefits you, your baby and even future generations.
- Support from health professionals trained in fertility and nutrition can help you prepare for conception and a healthy pregnancy.
- Whether you need help managing your weight, or are navigating PCOS, endometriosis, recurrent miscarriage, amenorrhoea, IBS, coeliac disease, early menopause, thyroid conditions such as Hashimoto's, or your partner has sperm quality concerns, Kat can provide evidence-based nutrition support tailored to your journey.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is pre-conception nutrition and why is it important?

Pre-conception nutrition refers to the dietary and lifestyle choices made before pregnancy to optimise health for both mother and future baby. A balanced, nutritious diet before conception helps regulate hormones, support healthy ovulation and support the quality of eggs and sperm. It can also help reduce the risk of complications such as pre-eclampsia and gestational diabetes and support the healthy development of your baby's organs and tissues. Nutrients such as folic acid, iron and omega-3 fats are particularly important in this period for reducing the risk of birth defects and supporting healthy development.

### When should I start?

It is generally recommended to focus on your diet and overall health several months before trying to conceive. This gives time to make dietary and lifestyle changes and to address any underlying health issues that could affect conception or pregnancy, such as excess weight, diabetes or thyroid disorders. For women, it helps ensure you are getting nutrients such as folic acid, iron and omega-3 fats. For men, it can support sperm quality and motility.

### Can nutrition help with medical conditions that affect fertility?

Yes. Conditions such as PCOS, endometriosis and thyroid disorders can affect hormone levels, ovulation and reproductive health, and diet and lifestyle can help manage them. In PCOS, a diet rich in whole grains, fruit, vegetables, lean proteins and healthy fats helps regulate insulin and supports regular ovulation. For endometriosis, an anti-inflammatory diet rich in fruit, vegetables and omega-3 fats may help reduce inflammation. A dietitian who specialises in fertility can build a plan tailored to your condition.
