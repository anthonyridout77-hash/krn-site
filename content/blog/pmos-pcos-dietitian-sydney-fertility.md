## What to look for in a PMOS dietitian in Sydney

Start with the credential. An Accredited Practising Dietitian has a university qualification in dietetics and keeps up ongoing professional development. You can check anyone's APD status through [Dietitians Australia](https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/). It takes a minute and it's worth doing.

Next, ask how often they work with PMOS and fertility. PMOS looks different from one woman to the next. Some women have irregular cycles, some have insulin resistance, some have both, and some have neither. You want someone who is comfortable with all of that, and with the extra layer of trying to conceive or preparing for IVF.

Then listen to how they talk about food. A good fit will talk about your whole eating pattern and your life, not one magic food or a strict list of banned ones. If you hear big promises, be wary. Nobody can promise a pregnancy, and nutrition is one part of the picture.

Finally, check the practical side. Will they write to your GP or fertility specialist? Do you get a written plan? Can your partner join? These details shape how supported you feel between appointments.

## What a dietitian does for PMOS when you're trying to conceive

A dietitian looks at the whole you. In a first appointment I go through what you eat across a normal week, your cycle history, recent blood results, any medications and supplements, your sleep, stress and activity, and where you are in the journey. Are you just starting to try, or are you about to start IVF?

From there, we work on a few things that matter most for you. That might be steadier energy across the day, building meals that keep you full, getting enough protein and fibre, or sorting out a supplement routine that actually makes sense. For many women with PMOS, insulin resistance sits underneath a lot of the symptoms, so we often look at how meals are put together and timed.

I also check in on the bits that get missed. Disordered eating and years of dieting are common in women with PMOS, so I take a gentle, non-restrictive approach. I'm a mum of three and I know real life gets in the way, so the plan has to fit the life you actually have.

Your dietitian supports your GP, fertility specialist, obstetrician or midwife. We never replace them.

## What the evidence says about eating with PMOS

The 2023 International Evidence-based Guideline for PCOS, led from Australia, recommends healthy eating and regular physical activity as part of care for all women with PMOS ([Teede et al., Human Reproduction](https://doi.org/10.1093/humrep/dead156)). It also says there is no single diet that suits everyone. The right eating pattern is one that is balanced, works for you and you can keep doing.

The [Australian Dietary Guidelines](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/) are a good foundation: plenty of vegetables, legumes, wholegrains, fruit, lean protein and healthy fats. Before conception, Australian advice is to take a supplement with folic acid and iodine, and to look at vitamin D, iron and B12 if levels are low. [Healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/planning-for-pregnancy) has a helpful overview.

Weight is a sensitive topic with PMOS. The guideline also recognises weight stigma and encourages care that is respectful and focused on health behaviours. That's how I work. If weight is something you want to talk about, we talk about it. If it isn't, we focus on everything else.

You can read more about the link between PMOS and getting pregnant in [Can a dietitian help with PCOS (PMOS) and getting pregnant?](/blogs/dietitian-pcos-pmos-getting-pregnant/), and about the supplement everyone asks about in [Inositol and PMOS](/blogs/inositol-pmos-pcos-dietitian-brisbane/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to see a dietitian in Sydney, or can I see one interstate?**
You can see any Accredited Practising Dietitian by telehealth, wherever they are based. What matters most is their qualification, their experience with PMOS and fertility, and whether they work well with your medical team.

**Is there a special PMOS diet for fertility?**
No single diet suits everyone with PMOS. The international guideline recommends healthy eating and regular activity, tailored to you. A dietitian helps you build a pattern that fits your life and your health picture.

**Should I take inositol for PMOS?**
Inositol is a common question. The evidence is still limited, so it is something to discuss with your dietitian and doctor rather than start on your own. I look at your whole situation before suggesting any supplement.

**Can I claim a Medicare rebate?**
A rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan. Your GP decides eligibility, so ask at your next appointment.

**Will you talk to my fertility specialist?**
With your permission, yes. I'm happy to write to your GP or fertility specialist so your nutrition plan sits alongside your medical care.
