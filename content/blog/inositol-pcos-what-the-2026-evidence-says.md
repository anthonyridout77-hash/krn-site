If you have polycystic ovary syndrome and you have spent any time in an online group, you have been told about inositol. It is the most consistently recommended supplement in that space, and it is the thing I am asked about most often in a first appointment for PCOS.

Two significant reviews landed in 2026, and between them they give a clearer answer than we have had before. The answer is more interesting than either “it works” or “it does not.”

## What the 2026 reviews looked at

In February 2026, *Frontiers in Endocrinology* published an umbrella review, a review of reviews, pulling together 13 meta-analyses of randomised controlled trials on inositol in PCOS, covering trials published up to August 2025. Separately, a 2026 scoping review in *Nutrients* by Habryka and colleagues screened 77 records and examined 13 studies on myo-inositol and fertility outcomes.

Between them they assessed hormonal markers, insulin and glucose measures, lipids, body measurements, and reproductive outcomes including ovulation, pregnancy and live birth.

## What they found

Compared with placebo or folic acid, inositol was associated with statistically significant differences on several measures. Luteinising hormone was lower by 3.43 IU/L. Fasting insulin was lower by 23.40 pmol/L. HOMA-IR, a measure of insulin resistance, was lower by 1.14. Sex hormone-binding globulin was higher by 36.72 nmol/L.

On the reproductive outcomes people actually care about, the pooled figures were striking. Ovulation rate carried a risk ratio of 2.75, and live births a risk ratio of 2.29.

Compared with metformin rather than placebo, most measures showed no significant difference, though inositol was associated with fewer gastrointestinal side effects.

## The part that gets left out of the forum post

Here is the sentence that rarely travels with those numbers.

Of the 85 outcomes the umbrella review graded for certainty, **not one was rated high quality.** Just under 19 per cent were moderate, 40 per cent low, and 41 per cent very low. On the AMSTAR-2 tool, which rates the quality of the reviews themselves, 23 per cent were high quality and 54 per cent were low.

![Chart showing that none of the 85 graded outcomes in the 2026 inositol umbrella review were rated high certainty](/images/wp/2026/08/krn-inositol-pcos-evidence-certainty.jpg)

The authors were direct about why. Doses varied. Forms varied: myo-inositol alone, myo-inositol with folic acid, D-chiro-inositol alone, and combinations. Durations varied. PCOS itself varied, because it is not one condition with one presentation. Roughly a quarter of the outcomes carried substantial statistical heterogeneity, and the authors wrote that the results “require extreme caution.”

The *Nutrients* review reached the same place from a different direction: no key outcome was consistently supported by truly high-quality evidence, and one large trial found that giving myo-inositol to pregnant women with PCOS did not reduce gestational diabetes or other pregnancy complications.

Both reviews concluded that inositol is promising, that it needs larger and more rigorous trials before it belongs in standard guidelines, and that it should sit alongside proven approaches rather than replace them.

## What I do with that in clinic

A supplement conversation is a real conversation, and it belongs with your GP, your specialist or your dietitian, because it depends on your bloods, your other medications, whether you are trying to conceive, and what you are already taking. I do not give doses in an article and you should be sceptical of anyone who does. That unpicking is a large part of what I do as a [PCOS dietitian in Brisbane](https://www.katridout.com.au/our-services/prenatal-nutrition-and-dietetics/polycystic-ovary-syndrome/).

What I can tell you is what the food side looks like, because it is the part that is under your control tonight and the part the research keeps circling back to. The measures inositol moved in those trials, fasting insulin, HOMA-IR and sex hormone-binding globulin, are the same measures that respond to the shape of what is on the plate.

In practice that means three things.

### Put protein and fibre in the same meal as the carbohydrate

Not less carbohydrate. Carbohydrate with company. A bowl of pasta on its own and a bowl of pasta with legumes, olive oil and a pile of greens behave differently, and the second one is not a smaller meal.

### Make legumes an ordinary weeknight food, not a health project

Tinned chickpeas, lentils and black beans are the cheapest, fastest source of the protein-and-fibre combination in the supermarket. Rinsed, drained, in the pan. This is the single change that moves the most for the least effort in a PCOS eating pattern.

![A bowl of legumes, couscous, cucumber and green leaves served together](/images/wp/2026/08/krn-iron-body2.jpg)

### Eat at roughly regular intervals

Long gaps followed by a very large meal are harder work than the same food spread across the day. This is unglamorous and it matters more than most supplements.
