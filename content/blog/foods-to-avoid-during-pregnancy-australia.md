When you find out you’re pregnant, the list of foods to avoid can feel endless. I’ve had three kids, and I remember standing at the deli counter wondering what on earth I was allowed to eat. I guess the key thing is this: the list is short and it has a reason behind it. In Australia, the foods to take care with are the ones that can carry listeria (soft cheeses, deli meats, pâté, pre-prepared salads, rockmelon, raw sprouts), fish high in mercury (shark or flake, swordfish, broadbill, marlin, orange roughy, catfish), raw or undercooked meat, poultry, seafood and eggs, unpasteurised dairy, and alcohol. Keep caffeine to 200mg a day. Most everyday foods are still on the menu.

*By Katrina Ridout, Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD), Brisbane. Reviewed October 2026.*

## Why does food safety matter more during pregnancy?

Pregnancy changes how your immune system responds, so infections like listeria, salmonella and toxoplasmosis can be more serious and can affect your baby. Listeria can be passed to the baby and is linked to miscarriage, stillbirth and premature birth. It is also unusual because it can keep growing in a fridge, even one kept below 5°C, according to [Food Standards Australia New Zealand](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/publications/listeriabrochuretext).

Salmonella can cause nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea and fever, and in rare cases miscarriage. Toxoplasmosis can be found in raw meat, unwashed vegetables and cat faeces and can harm a baby’s brain and eyes. If you have a cat, wear gloves for the litter tray, or better still, hand that job to hubby for a while.

This is about a small number of higher-risk foods, not about eating fearfully. Think of it as prevention, not cure: know which foods deserve extra attention, and keep enjoying the rest.

## Which foods carry the highest listeria risk in Australia?

The main listeria-risk foods are soft cheeses (brie, camembert, ricotta, feta and blue cheese) unless cooked, pâté and meat spreads, deli meats such as ham and salami, cold cooked chicken, pre-prepared or pre-packaged salads and fruit salad, store-bought sushi, rockmelon, and raw or lightly cooked sprouts, according to the [NSW Food Authority](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-risk-you/pregnancy/foods-to-eat-or-avoid-when-pregnant).

The reason is practical: a long fridge life gives listeria time to grow, and soft cheese, processed meats and pre-cut fruit are more likely to be contaminated. The workaround is heat. Soft cheeses can be eaten if cooked to at least 75°C and eaten straight away, according to [Pregnancy, Birth and Baby](https://www.pregnancybirthbaby.org.au/pregnancy/nutrition-and-lifestyle/foods-to-avoid-when-pregnant). So a baked ricotta dish or a hot cheesy pasta bake is fine. Deli meats should also be cooked to at least 75°C and eaten soon afterwards.

Hard cheeses such as cheddar, parmesan, edam and tasty are fine, stored in the fridge and within date, according to the [NSW Food Authority](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-risk-you/pregnancy/foods-to-eat-or-avoid-when-pregnant). Pasteurised milk, yoghurt and cream are also fine when stored correctly and used within date.

## Which fish are safe, and which should you limit for mercury?

Please don’t hear the word mercury and stop eating fish. Fish gives you omega-3 for baby’s brain and eyes, and it’s worth keeping in two to three times a week. It’s the big predatory fish that need limiting: shark (flake), marlin, broadbill, swordfish, orange roughy (deep sea perch) and catfish, according to [Food Standards Australia New Zealand](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/consumer/chemicals/mercury). Tinned tuna is a low-mercury option, so eat the fish you actually like.

The specific limits are: no more than 150g of shark (flake), swordfish, broadbill or marlin in a fortnight, with no other fish that fortnight, according to [Pregnancy, Birth and Baby](https://www.pregnancybirthbaby.org.au/pregnancy/nutrition-and-lifestyle/foods-to-avoid-when-pregnant). Orange roughy or catfish no more than once a week, with no other fish that week. Other fish or seafood two to three times a week.

All fish and seafood should be cooked to at least 63°C and eaten hot, according to [Pregnancy, Birth and Baby](https://www.pregnancybirthbaby.org.au/pregnancy/nutrition-and-lifestyle/foods-to-avoid-when-pregnant). Raw seafood and ready-to-eat chilled peeled prawns should be avoided because they can carry harmful viruses and bacteria, according to the [NSW Food Authority](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-risk-you/pregnancy/foods-to-eat-or-avoid-when-pregnant).

## Can you eat eggs, meat and chicken during pregnancy?

Yes, eggs, meat and chicken are all fine when they are cooked properly. Raw and lightly cooked eggs should be avoided because of salmonella, so cook eggs until the whites and yolks are firm, according to [Pregnancy, Birth and Baby](https://www.pregnancybirthbaby.org.au/pregnancy/nutrition-and-lifestyle/foods-to-avoid-when-pregnant). Skip raw egg foods like home-made mayonnaise, aioli, chocolate mousse and cake or pancake batter (yes, even licking the bowl).

All meat and poultry should be cooked to at least 75°C and eaten hot. Raw meat can carry salmonella, listeria, campylobacter and E. coli, according to the [NSW Food Authority](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-risk-you/pregnancy/foods-to-eat-or-avoid-when-pregnant).

Processed meats such as ham, salami and luncheon should be avoided unless thoroughly cooked to at least 75°C and eaten soon afterwards. A hot, freshly cooked roast chicken is fine if eaten hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, then reheat until steaming hot and eat within 24 hours.

## What about dairy, caffeine and alcohol?

Unpasteurised (raw) dairy should not be consumed, but pasteurised milk, cream, yoghurt and hard cheese are fine, according to the [NSW Food Authority](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-risk-you/pregnancy/foods-to-eat-or-avoid-when-pregnant). Soft serve and fried ice cream are worth skipping for now, while packaged ice cream kept frozen is okay.

Caffeine is worth counting rather than cutting. The cap is around 200mg a day, which is roughly one to two espresso coffees, a few instant coffees, or several cups of tea, according to [Food Standards Australia New Zealand](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/consumer/nutrition/pregnancy/pregnancy-and-healthy-eating). A large café coffee can be a double shot, so count the shots, not the cups.

No amount of alcohol is known to be safe in pregnancy, and not drinking is the safest option for women who are pregnant, planning a pregnancy or breastfeeding, according to [Eat For Health](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-09/n55h_healthy_eating_during_pregnancy_0_0.pdf). Vitamin A is the other one to watch: choose a multivitamin made for pregnancy rather than a general one that may contain retinol, and keep liver and liver pâté to small, occasional amounts.

## How do you store and reheat food safely when pregnant?

Keep the fridge at 5°C or below, put leftovers in the fridge within 2 hours, eat them within 24 hours and reheat until steaming hot all the way through. Listeria is killed by heat, so thorough cooking and reheating is the key step, according to [Food Standards Australia New Zealand](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/publications/listeriabrochuretext).

Frozen vegetables should be cooked rather than eaten straight from the bag, according to the [NSW Food Authority](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-risk-you/pregnancy/foods-to-eat-or-avoid-when-pregnant). Home-made salads are fine if you wash the ingredients well just before eating, with leftovers refrigerated and used within a day.

Whole fresh fruit and vegetables are fine when washed well. Australian tap water is generally safe unless a boil water alert is issued, in which case bring water to a rolling boil for at least 1 minute, according to [Pregnancy, Birth and Baby](https://www.pregnancybirthbaby.org.au/pregnancy/nutrition-and-lifestyle/foods-to-avoid-when-pregnant). And the good news for later: once bubs arrives, most of these rules ease, and the pre-made and pre-cut foods come back.

## Key Takeaways

* Listeria is the main reason behind most pregnancy food rules in Australia, and it can grow even in a fridge kept below 5°C.
* Soft cheeses, deli meats, pâté, pre-prepared salads, store-bought sushi, rockmelon and raw sprouts are the higher-risk listeria foods unless cooked and eaten hot.
* Fish is worth eating two to three times a week, but shark (flake), swordfish, broadbill, marlin, orange roughy and catfish need limiting for mercury.
* Cook all meat, poultry and seafood thoroughly and eat it hot, and avoid raw or lightly cooked eggs.
* Keep caffeine at or below about 200mg a day, and no amount of alcohol is known to be safe in pregnancy.
* Fridge at 5°C or below, leftovers within 24 hours, and reheating until steaming hot are the habits that reduce risk most.
