## Why PMOS and fertility nutrition go together

PMOS is common. It can affect cycles, ovulation, insulin, energy, mood and weight. If you are trying to fall pregnant, it can feel like there is a lot to sort out at once.

The international PCOS guideline, published in 2023, recommends healthy eating and physical activity as part of first-line care. That includes women who are trying to conceive. Food is not the whole answer, and it does not replace your doctor. But it is one part of your care that you get to shape every day. My article on [whether a dietitian can help with PCOS and getting pregnant](/blogs/dietitian-pcos-pmos-getting-pregnant/) goes into the evidence in more detail.

I have worked as a dietitian for more than 20 years, and I am a mum of three. A lot of my week is spent with women who have PMOS and want a plan that fits a busy life.

## What to look for in a PMOS fertility dietitian

Here is what I would check before booking anyone, including me.

- **The APD credential.** Accredited Practising Dietitians are university-trained and recognised by Dietitians Australia. You can check on their find an APD directory.
- **Regular PMOS and fertility work.** Ask how much of their week is spent with women who have PMOS and are trying to conceive.
- **No crash diets.** Very restrictive plans are hard to keep up and can make things harder, not easier. Look for someone who builds a pattern you can live with.
- **Plans built on your results.** Your bloods, cycle history and medications should shape the plan.
- **A careful approach to supplements.** Inositol, vitamin D and other supplements come up a lot with PMOS. You want someone who looks at the evidence and your situation first.
- **Respect for your medical team.** Your GP, gynaecologist or fertility specialist stay in charge of your medical care.

## What a dietitian does for PMOS when you are trying to conceive

Every woman with PMOS is different. Some have regular cycles, some have none. Some have insulin resistance, some do not. So the plan starts with you.

**Eating for steady blood sugar.** We look at how your meals are built across the day. Fibre, protein, healthy fats and the type of carbohydrate all matter. You do not need to cut out carbs. We find a balance that keeps you full and gives you energy.

**Preconception nutrients.** The NHMRC recommends a folic acid supplement of at least 400 micrograms a day for at least one month before conception and through the first three months of pregnancy, plus 150 micrograms of iodine a day. Some women need a higher folate dose, and that is a conversation for your GP. If you have a thyroid condition, check with your doctor before taking iodine.

**Supplements.** I look at what you already take and what your bloods show. Then I give you an individualised supplement plan, so you are not guessing or doubling up. My article on [inositol and PMOS](/blogs/inositol-pmos-pcos-dietitian-brisbane/) explains what the guideline says about that one.

**Weight, without shame.** Some women with PMOS want to talk about weight, and some do not. Both are fine. We focus on habits you can keep, not a number on the scales.

**Around medication or fertility treatment.** If you are taking metformin, using ovulation induction or starting IVF, I work around the plan your doctor has set. Nutrition supports that care and never replaces it.

**Partners.** Sperm health is linked to diet and lifestyle too. Partners are welcome to join.

## Working alongside your doctors

You keep your GP, gynaecologist, endocrinologist or fertility clinic. With your permission, I can send a summary of your plan to your GP so everyone is on the same page.

If you have heavy bleeding, severe pain, or you feel unwell, contact your GP or clinic straight away. Those are not questions to save for a dietitian.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is PMOS the same as PCOS?

Yes. PMOS is the new name for the condition most people know as PCOS. The care and the evidence are the same. You will see both names for a while.

### Do I have to cut out carbs if I have PMOS?

No. Carbohydrates can be part of a healthy PMOS eating pattern. The type, the amount and what you pair them with matter more than cutting them out.

### Do I need a referral to see you?

No referral is needed to book privately. If you want to explore a Medicare rebate, talk to your GP first. A rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan, and the GP decides eligibility.

### Can I see you by telehealth if I live outside Brisbane?

Yes. I see women across Australia by telehealth video. The consult covers the same ground as an in-person visit in Milton.

### Should I take inositol for PMOS?

It depends on you. The 2023 guideline says the evidence for inositol is still limited. We look at your situation together, and your doctor is part of that decision.

### What should I bring to my first appointment?

Recent blood results if you have them, a list of medications and supplements, and a rough idea of your cycles. A note of what you ate over a couple of normal days helps too.
