## What to look for in a telehealth pregnancy dietitian

Wherever you book, these are the things I would check.

- **The APD credential.** Accredited Practising Dietitians are listed on the Dietitians Australia find an APD directory. Ask for the APD number if it is not shown.
- **Regular pregnancy work.** Pregnancy nutrition has its own rules around food safety, supplements and weight gain. Ask how much of their week is spent on it.
- **A proper first consult.** A short call cannot cover your history, your bloods, your supplements and your eating. Look for a consult with enough time.
- **A written plan you keep.** You should not have to remember everything from a video call.
- **They work with your care team.** Your GP, obstetrician or midwife stays in charge of your medical care.
- **Clear information about costs before you book.**

## What a dietitian does in pregnancy

A pregnancy dietitian helps you eat well for you and your baby in a way that fits your real life. That can mean:

- getting through nausea and food aversions in the first trimester
- checking iron, iodine, folate, choline, vitamin D and omega-3 intake
- sorting out which supplements you actually need
- planning meals around vegetarian, vegan, cultural or allergy needs
- understanding healthy weight gain for your body
- managing reflux, constipation and tiredness with food
- support around gestational diabetes, PMOS (previously called PCOS) or a history of pregnancy complications

Your needs change as you go. My article on [how your nutritional needs change in each trimester](/blogs/how-your-nutritional-needs-change-in-each-trimester/) walks through this.

## How a telehealth consult works

**Booking.** You start with a free 15-minute call so we can check I am the right fit. Then you book your first consult at a time that suits you.

**Before we meet.** I send a short intake form. It helps if you have your recent blood results, a list of your supplements and medications, and a rough idea of a normal day of eating. A photo of your supplement labels is very useful.

**The video call.** Your first consult is 75 minutes. You join from your phone, tablet or computer. You can sit on the couch, feed a toddler or put your feet up. We talk through your history, your pregnancy so far, your bloods and how you are eating. Then we build your plan together.

**After the call.** Within 2 business days you receive a written plan and an individualised supplement plan. The supplement plan looks at what you already take and what your pregnancy needs, so you are not guessing or doubling up.

**Follow-ups.** These are shorter and practical. We adjust the plan as your pregnancy changes, often around the second trimester and again in the last few weeks.

## Working with your care team

Nutrition supports your GP, obstetrician, midwife or hospital team and never replaces them. With your permission, I can send a summary of your plan to your GP.

Please contact your doctor, midwife or hospital straight away if you feel unwell, have bleeding or pain, or are worried about your baby's movements. Those are never questions to save for a dietitian.

## Fees and rebates

**Referral.** You do not need a referral to see a dietitian privately.

**Medicare.** A rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan; the GP decides eligibility. My [Medicare rebate guide](/blogs/medicare-rebate-dietitian-australia/) explains how this works.

**Private health.** Most private health extras cover APDs. Check your own policy and limits with your insurer, including whether it covers telehealth consults.

Current fees are confirmed when you book. You are welcome to ask about them on the free call.

## When to book

Any stage of pregnancy is fine. Early is often most helpful, because supplements and food safety matter from the first weeks. It is also worth booking if:

- nausea means you are living on crackers
- your bloods show low iron or vitamin D
- you follow a vegetarian, vegan or restricted diet
- you have PMOS, a past pregnancy with gestational diabetes, or another health condition
- you are unsure which supplements to take

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I see a pregnancy dietitian by telehealth if I do not live in Brisbane?

Yes. I see women across Australia by telehealth video, in every state and territory. It covers the same ground as an in-person consult in Milton.

### What do I need for a telehealth consult?

A phone, tablet or computer with a camera, a reasonable internet connection and a quiet-ish spot. Have your blood results and supplement list handy if you can.

### Do I need a referral?

No referral is needed to book privately. If you want to explore a Medicare rebate, talk to your GP first. The GP decides eligibility.

### Which supplements do I need in pregnancy?

It depends on you. The NHMRC recommends folic acid in early pregnancy and 150 micrograms of iodine a day for most pregnant women. If you have a thyroid condition, check with your doctor before taking iodine. My [iodine in pregnancy article](/blogs/iodine-supplement-pregnancy-australia/) explains more.

### How many appointments will I need?

Some women need one consult and a check-in. Others like follow-ups through each trimester. We decide together based on what you need.

### Does private health cover telehealth dietitian consults?

Many extras policies cover APD consults, and many include telehealth. Check your own policy and limits with your insurer.
