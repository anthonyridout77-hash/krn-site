<h2 id="is-the-dietitian-an-apd">Is the dietitian an Accredited Practising Dietitian?</h2>
<p>Start here. Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) is the credential run by Dietitians Australia. An APD has completed accredited university training in dietetics, keeps up ongoing professional development and works to evidence based practice standards. Fertility nutrition sits right alongside medical care, so you want advice grounded in research rather than trends. You can check anyone&#8217;s credential on the <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/find-an-apd">Dietitians Australia Find an APD</a> search.</p>
<h2 id="do-they-work-with-your-situation">Do they work with your situation?</h2>
<p>Ask whether the dietitian regularly supports people in your exact spot. That might be IVF preparation, PMOS (previously called PCOS), endometriosis, recurrent loss, or simply wanting to get your body ready in the months before you start trying. Ask too whether they can see you as a couple. Healthdirect notes that smoking is linked to infertility in both males and females, and sperm health is part of the picture for a lot of couples, so nutrition for both partners is worth talking about.</p>
<p>A few practical questions help as well:</p>
<ul>
<li>What do I need to bring? Recent bloods, a photo of your supplements and a jotted typical day are useful.</li>
<li>Will I get a written plan, and how soon after the consult?</li>
<li>How is supplement advice matched to my bloods rather than handed out as a generic list?</li>
<li>How often are reviews, and can I email between them?</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="how-do-they-work-with-your-fertility-clinic-and-gp">How do they work with your fertility clinic and GP?</h2>
<p>Nutrition supports medical care. It doesn&#8217;t replace it. A good dietitian will ask early who holds your care, follow your specialist&#8217;s plan, and with your permission write back to your GP or clinic so everyone is on the same page. If a dietitian never asks about your medical team, that&#8217;s worth noticing.</p>
<h2 id="what-does-the-evidence-say-about-nutrition-and-fertility">What does the evidence say about nutrition and fertility?</h2>
<p>What we do know is that the research on diet and fertility is mostly observational, and it points to overall eating patterns rather than any single wonder food. A varied, Mediterranean style way of eating with plenty of vegetables, wholegrains, legumes, nuts and fish keeps coming up, and there&#8217;s no one food that changes everything.</p>
<p>Some advice is clear cut. Healthdirect advises most people planning a pregnancy to take 400 micrograms of folic acid every day, starting 12 weeks before trying to conceive and continuing until more than 12 weeks pregnant, and 150 micrograms of iodine a day (speak with your doctor first if you have a thyroid condition). It also advises stopping alcohol when you&#8217;re planning a pregnancy.</p>
<p>Keep expectations realistic. No diet can promise a pregnancy, and no dietitian should tell you otherwise. What good nutrition can do is close your nutrition gaps, build your reserves and support your body through treatment.</p>
<h2 id="in-person-or-telehealth">In person or telehealth?</h2>
<p>It depends on your schedule and how you like to work. Some people love sitting in a clinic room. Others find a video call much easier to fit around work, appointments and everything else IVF throws at you. Telehealth also means you can choose a dietitian for their experience rather than their postcode. If you&#8217;re weighing it up, our guide to <a href="/blogs/do-you-need-fertility-dietitian-melbourne/">choosing a fertility dietitian in Melbourne</a> goes deeper into what a fertility dietitian does.</p>
<h2 id="what-about-medicare">What about Medicare?</h2>
<p>A Medicare rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan. Eligibility isn&#8217;t automatic, so ask your GP whether a plan suits your situation. Private health extras cover varies by fund and policy too. Our article on <a href="/blogs/how-much-does-a-dietitian-cost-australia/">help with the cost of seeing a dietitian</a> walks through how it works.</p>

<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Can I see a Brisbane dietitian by telehealth from Melbourne?</h3>
<p>Yes. Kat works with women Australia wide by telehealth, including Melbourne.</p>
<h3>Do I need a GP referral to see a dietitian?</h3>
<p>No, you can book directly. A GP Chronic Condition Management Plan may make a Medicare rebate available for eligible patients, but you don&#8217;t need one to book.</p>
<h3>Is it worth seeing a fertility dietitian before IVF?</h3>
<p>Many people find individual nutrition support useful in the months before a cycle, because that&#8217;s when there&#8217;s time to build reserves. It isn&#8217;t a requirement, and it always sits alongside your fertility specialist&#8217;s care.</p>
