<h2 id="does-private-health-insurance-cover-dietitian-appointments">Does private health insurance cover dietitian appointments?</h2>
<p>Yes, most private health funds cover visits to Accredited Practising Dietitians in private practice, according to <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/seeing-dietitian/help-costs">Dietitians Australia</a>. This cover usually sits under extras cover, which some funds call ancillary cover, rather than hospital cover.</p>
<p>You do not need a GP referral to claim a dietitian appointment through private health insurance. That is the big difference from the Medicare pathway, where a GP plan comes first.</p>
<p>The amount you get back, your annual limit and any waiting periods are set by each fund and each policy. Two people who both hold extras cover can receive quite different amounts back for the same appointment. Check your fund&#8217;s extras table for dietetics specifically, not just &#8220;allied health&#8221;, because some funds list dietetics as its own item with its own benefit.</p>
<h2 id="what-should-i-check-in-my-extras-cover-before-booking">What should I check in my extras cover before booking?</h2>
<p>A simple little checklist before you book:</p>
<ul>
<li>Is dietetics a listed service on your policy?</li>
<li>What is the benefit per appointment?</li>
<li>What is your annual limit per person, and how much of it is left?</li>
<li>Does a waiting period apply if your extras cover is new?</li>
<li>Do telehealth appointments attract the same benefit as in-person visits?</li>
</ul>
<p>The benefit is usually a set amount per visit rather than a percentage of the appointment cost, so what you pay yourself can vary between practices, as <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/seeing-dietitian/help-costs">Dietitians Australia</a> explains.</p>
<p>Extras limits reset each year and are often shared across several allied health services. If you&#8217;ve already used physio or podiatry visits this year, your remaining dietitian benefit may be lower than the headline limit suggests.</p>
<h2 id="how-is-private-health-cover-different-from-the-medicare-pathway">How is private health cover different from the Medicare pathway?</h2>
<p>The main difference is how you get there. For Medicare, you need a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan (GPCCMP) for a chronic condition expected to last at least six months. Private health extras need no referral and no diagnosis.</p>
<p>Under a GPCCMP, Medicare supports a limited number of individual allied health sessions each calendar year, shared across all your allied health providers. Your GP can tell you how many you have left.</p>
<p>You generally can&#8217;t use both for the same appointment. It&#8217;s worth asking which pathway suits you better before you book, because the answer depends on your fund and your remaining Medicare sessions.</p>
<h2 id="how-much-will-i-get-back-from-my-fund">How much will I get back from my fund?</h2>
<p>It depends on your fund. Funds set their own benefits and limits, so the only reliable figure is the one in your own policy documents.</p>
<p>A Medicare rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan. Services Australia or your GP can confirm the current details for you.</p>
<p>Call your fund, ask about &#8220;dietetics&#8221;, and ask for the benefit per visit and your remaining annual limit. It takes a few minutes and takes the guesswork out of it.</p>
<h2 id="can-i-use-private-health-extras-and-medicare-together">Can I use private health extras and Medicare together?</h2>
<p>Usually not for the same appointment. Private health insurance and Medicare are separate pathways, and the standard advice is to claim one per appointment rather than both.</p>
<p>Some funds ask you to choose which pathway you&#8217;re claiming under, so ask your fund and the practice how they process claims. This is about knowing your options, not squeezing two rebates out of one visit.</p>
<h2 id="what-if-i-dont-have-private-health-insurance-or-a-care-plan">What if I don&#8217;t have private health insurance or a care plan?</h2>
<p>You can still see a dietitian without any referral. No referral is needed to see a dietitian privately.</p>
<p>Other schemes may help too. DVA card holders may be covered for dietetic services, NDIS participants may be able to access dietetic funding through their plan, and some compensation schemes such as WorkSafe Queensland, Comcare and TAC in Victoria may cover dietitian visits, as outlined by <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/seeing-dietitian/help-costs">Dietitians Australia</a>.</p>
<h2 id="how-do-i-claim-through-my-private-health-fund">How do I claim through my private health fund?</h2>
<p>Confirm your cover and remaining limit with your fund, book with an Accredited Practising Dietitian, then claim through your fund&#8217;s app or card. Keep your receipts in case your fund asks for them later.</p>
<p>Ask the practice at booking how they handle health fund claims. That way there are no surprises on the day.</p>
