If you are pregnant or planning a pregnancy in Sydney, you might be wondering whether a pregnancy dietitian is worth it, and whether they need to be local. Lots of women find it really helpful, and no, they don’t need to be in Sydney. I see women all over Australia by telehealth from Brisbane. A pregnancy dietitian supports your GP, obstetrician or midwife. We never replace them.

*By Katrina Ridout, Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD), Brisbane. Reviewed October 2026.*

## What does a pregnancy dietitian actually do?

A pregnancy dietitian turns the evidence into an eating and supplement plan that fits you, from preconception through each trimester and into the postpartum months. An Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) is trained to look at what you are eating now, spot the gaps and suggest practical changes that work with your symptoms and your life.

Common reasons women come to see me include nausea and food aversions, low iron, working out which supplements they actually need, gestational diabetes, reflux, and eating well on broken sleep once the baby arrives.

It’s worth knowing the difference between a dietitian and a nutritionist. Only Accredited Practising Dietitians can provide Medicare-rebated dietitian care under a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan.

## What nutrition matters most in each trimester?

In the first trimester, folate and iodine do a lot of the heavy lifting for your baby’s early development, and nausea often decides what is realistic. This is the time for small, frequent meals and whatever plain foods you can manage. Don’t beat yourself up if the veg goes out the window for a few weeks.

In the second trimester, iron and calcium needs rise, and gestational diabetes screening usually happens between 24 and 28 weeks. [Healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/gestational-diabetes) explains what that involves.

In the third trimester, the focus moves to steady energy, fibre and fluids for bowel comfort, managing reflux with smaller meals, and building a few freezer meals for the weeks after birth. Targets for each nutrient are individual, so I work from your bloods rather than a generic chart.

## Food safety in pregnancy

Pregnancy food safety is mostly about listeria: skipping pre-prepared salads, having deli meats and soft cheeses only when cooked until steaming hot, and reheating leftovers properly. [Food Standards Australia New Zealand](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/) has the full list. It sounds like a lot, but once you know the handful of swaps it becomes second nature.

## When should you book in with a pregnancy dietitian?

The window most often skipped is preconception, ideally a few months before trying, so your stores of iron, folate and iodine are in good shape. Early pregnancy is a good time if nausea is making eating hard or you are unsure about supplements. A gestational diabetes diagnosis is a very common reason to book, and the postpartum months are a great time too, especially if your own meals have slipped while you look after everyone else.

## How to choose a pregnancy dietitian in Sydney

Look first for the Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) credential. You can check it through [Dietitians Australia](https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/). It only takes a minute, and it tells you the person has a recognised dietetics degree and keeps up ongoing professional development.

Next, look for an evidence-based approach. A good fit will explain the why behind their advice and talk about overall eating patterns rather than hero foods, detoxes or expensive protocols. If something comes with a big promise about outcomes, that promise is usually running ahead of the evidence.

Then think about what matters for you. If you have gestational diabetes, PMOS, a vegetarian diet, a twin pregnancy, or severe nausea, it helps to see someone who works with these regularly. Ask how often they see this, and whether they will talk to your doctors.

Finally, the practical things count. How long is the first consult? Do you get a written plan? Can your partner come along? Is telehealth an option for reviews? These small details make a big difference to how supported you feel.
