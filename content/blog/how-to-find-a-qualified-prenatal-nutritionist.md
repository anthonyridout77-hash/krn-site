**Quick answer:** In Australia, look for an **Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD)** who specialises in pregnancy. “Nutritionist” isn’t a protected title, so anyone can use it. APD is the credential Medicare, the DVA and private health funds recognise. Search the Dietitians Australia “Find an Accredited Practising Dietitian” directory by postcode and filter for pregnancy or women’s health. Telehealth means you can see a specialist anywhere in Australia.

*By Katrina Ridout, Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD), Brisbane. Reviewed September 2026.*

## Step 1: Check the credential, not the title

A prenatal “nutritionist” might hold a university degree, or might have done a short online course. An APD has:

* an accredited university degree in dietetics
* supervised clinical training
* ongoing professional development each year, overseen by Dietitians Australia

This matters in pregnancy. Advice on gestational diabetes, iron deficiency, nausea, PMOS (previously called PCOS) or food safety needs a clinical background.

## Step 2: Search the right places

1. **Dietitians Australia “Find an APD” directory.** Search by suburb or postcode and choose “pregnancy” or “women’s health” as the area of practice.
2. **Your GP, obstetrician or midwife.** They often refer to dietitians they know, and a referral may make you eligible for Medicare rebates.
3. **Your fertility clinic,** if you’re having IVF. Many clinics work alongside fertility dietitians.
4. **Telehealth.** You aren’t limited to your suburb. A pregnancy-focused APD can see you by video anywhere in Australia.

## Step 3: Ask these questions before booking

* Are you an Accredited Practising Dietitian?
* How much of your work is preconception, pregnancy or postpartum?
* Do you support gestational diabetes, PMOS, endometriosis or IVF, if relevant to you?
* Do you offer telehealth?
* What do fees cost, and can I claim Medicare or private health?

## Costs and rebates

* **Medicare:** eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan (GPCCMP) can claim a rebate for up to 5 allied health visits a year. Your GP decides eligibility.
* **Private health:** most extras policies cover dietetics with an APD. You can’t claim Medicare and private health for the same appointment.

## FAQ

**Is a prenatal nutritionist the same as a dietitian?**  
Not necessarily. All APDs are qualified in nutrition, but not every nutritionist is a dietitian. For medical conditions in pregnancy, choose an APD.

**Do I need a referral to see a pregnancy dietitian?**  
No. You only need a GP referral if you want to claim Medicare.

**Can I see a prenatal dietitian online?**  
Yes. Telehealth consults work well for pregnancy nutrition, and rebates can apply to eligible telehealth consults too.
