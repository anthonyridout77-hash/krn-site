*By Katrina Ridout, Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD), Brisbane. Reviewed November 2026.*

## First, you did not cause this

Pregnancy hormones make insulin work less efficiently. For some women that pushes blood glucose higher than is healthy for pregnancy. It is common, and it is not a verdict on how you have been eating.

I am a dietitian and a mum of three, and I know how fast this diagnosis can feel. One day you are choosing a pram. The next you are pricking your finger and looking suspiciously at a banana.

If you are in the first few days, start with my guide on [what to do when you have just been diagnosed](/blogs/gestational-diabetes-just-diagnosed-what-now/).

## Do you need a private dietitian if your hospital has one?

Many Sydney women are looked after through a hospital antenatal or diabetes clinic, and some will see a hospital dietitian there. If that is you, please use that service. It is good care and it is connected to your team.

A private dietitian can be useful when you want more time, or when you are:

- Waiting for your first hospital appointment and unsure what to eat now
- Seeing a private obstetrician without a dietitian attached
- Managing vegetarian eating, cultural food traditions, nausea or food aversions
- Doing shift work, or juggling toddlers and irregular meals
- Finding your readings hard to make sense of

Tell your hospital team if you see someone privately, so everyone is working from the same page.

## What to look for

Here is what I would check if I were choosing for myself.

- **The APD credential.** You can confirm it through the Dietitians Australia find a dietitian directory.
- **Regular pregnancy work.** Ask how much of their week is gestational diabetes and pregnancy nutrition.
- **Respect for your team's targets.** Your dietitian should work within the blood glucose targets set by your doctor or clinic, not invent new ones.
- **No extreme carbohydrate cutting.** You and your baby need carbohydrate. Be cautious of anyone who tells you to remove it.
- **Willingness to read your numbers.** A plan should respond to your actual readings.
- **Clear information about costs and rebates before you book.**

## What a dietitian does for gestational diabetes

In our first consult I look at your glucose test results, your readings so far, your medications and supplements, your weight history if you are comfortable sharing it, and what a normal day looks like for you.

Then we work on the practical parts:

- How to spread carbohydrate across meals and snacks
- Pairing carbohydrate with protein and fibre
- Choosing the type and portion of carbohydrate that suits your readings
- What to do about a high fasting number, and which questions to take to your team
- Breakfasts you can face, because breakfast is often the tricky one
- Snacks for work, night shifts and the school run
- Making sure you are still eating enough for pregnancy

If you want ideas before we meet, my [gestational diabetes meal plan guide](/blogs/gestational-diabetes-meal-plan-guide/) is a good place to start.

Some women need metformin or insulin as well as food changes. That is not failure. Late in pregnancy, hormones can push glucose up however carefully you eat. Medication decisions always sit with your doctor. Your food plan is then adjusted to fit.

The diagnostic thresholds also changed in Australia in 2025, and services have moved across at different speeds. I explain that in [why the gestational diabetes test numbers changed](/blogs/gestational-diabetes-test-numbers-changed-adips-2025/). Ask your Sydney team which criteria they use.

## Working alongside your Sydney care team

Your obstetrician, midwife, GP, hospital clinic or diabetes educator stays in charge of your medical care. With your permission, I can send a summary so everyone knows what we are working on.

Please contact your doctor, midwife or hospital straight away if you are unwell, if your readings are outside the range they have asked you to report, or if you are worried about your baby's movements. Those are never questions to save for a dietitian.

Your diabetes educator or doctor can also register you with the National Diabetes Services Scheme, which gives access to subsidised testing supplies and information.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Brisbane dietitian help if I am having my baby in Sydney?

Yes. By telehealth video I can review your readings and results and build a plan with you from anywhere in Australia. Your medical care stays with your Sydney team.

### Do I need a referral?

No referral is needed to book privately. If you want to explore a Medicare rebate, talk to your GP first. A rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan, and the GP decides eligibility.

### Do I have to stop eating carbs?

No. Carbohydrate is an important part of pregnancy eating. We work on the type, the amount and how it is spread across your day.

### What should I have ready for my first appointment?

Your glucose test results, your blood glucose readings, a list of medications and supplements, and any targets your team has given you. If some of it comes later, that is fine.

### Will gestational diabetes go away after birth?

For most women glucose levels return to normal after birth. You will usually be asked to have a repeat glucose test, and your risk of type 2 diabetes later in life is higher, so ongoing check-ups with your GP matter.
