I guess the key thing is getting good support early, from someone who suits you. <h2 id="what-does-a-gestational-diabetes-dietitian-actually-do">What does a gestational diabetes dietitian actually do?</h2>
<p>A gestational diabetes dietitian provides individualised nutrition support, which is the first-line management for gestational diabetes, alongside your GP, obstetrician, midwife and diabetes educator. <a href="https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/gestational-diabetes">Healthdirect</a> describes healthy eating and physical activity as the starting point for managing blood glucose in pregnancy, with medicine added if it is needed.</p>
<p>The work is practical and tailored to your life. A dietitian helps you build an eating pattern that spreads carbohydrate across the day, supports meal timing and fits your routine. They also help you make sense of your blood glucose readings, so the plan can be adjusted as your pregnancy progresses.</p>
<p>A dietitian supports your care team and never replaces medical or obstetric care. The credential to look for is Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD). APDs are university-qualified and meet national standards for education and ongoing practice, set by <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/">Dietitians Australia</a>.</p>
<h2 id="when-should-you-see-a-dietitian-after-a-gestational-diabetes-diagnosis">When should you see a dietitian after a gestational diabetes diagnosis?</h2>
<p>Promptly after diagnosis is the usual answer. Gestational diabetes is usually picked up on the oral glucose tolerance test, often between 24 and 28 weeks, so support is most useful soon after those results come back.</p>
<p>Many people have an initial consultation and then a few reviews through the rest of the pregnancy, because blood glucose needs shift as baby grows. Your GP, midwife or hospital team can refer you. A Medicare rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan, so it is worth asking your GP.</p>
<p>Early support is practical, not alarming. It is about intervening early and building a plan that fits your life, your meals and your pregnancy, rather than waiting until readings are harder to manage. Prevention, not cure.</p>
<h2 id="what-should-you-look-for-when-choosing-a-gestational-diabetes-dietitian-in-brisbane">What should you look for when choosing a gestational diabetes dietitian in Brisbane?</h2>
<p>Look for an Accredited Practising Dietitian with specific experience in pregnancy and gestational diabetes. The APD credential confirms university-level training, and pregnancy experience means the dietitian understands how blood glucose changes across the trimesters.</p>
<p>Practical fit matters too. Check whether the dietitian offers in-person consults in Brisbane, telehealth if you prefer it or live outside the city, and appointment times that work around work, appointments and other kids.</p>
<p>Ask how care is structured. A useful service includes an initial consult plus reviews, a written plan and individualised supplement guidance where appropriate. Also ask whether the dietitian works alongside your hospital or clinic team and writes back to your doctor, because gestational diabetes care is a team effort.</p>

Then we start from what you already do. You don&#8217;t eat badly, you&#8217;re usually just missing a few key things. We look at how to pair carbohydrate with protein and fibre, eating small and often, and simple little switches that keep readings steady without making you feel deprived. <h2 id="does-telehealth-work-as-well-as-in-person-for-gestational-diabetes-nutrition">Does telehealth work as well as in-person for gestational diabetes nutrition?</h2>
<p>Yes, telehealth can work well for gestational diabetes nutrition support, and it suits women outside Brisbane or short on time. The quality of the plan and the follow-up matters far more than whether you are in the room.</p>
<p>Written plans and reviews can be delivered remotely, and blood glucose logs can be shared with your dietitian between appointments. You can leave your Ugg boots on, and kids in the background are completely fine.</p>
<p>Before a telehealth appointment, check that you have reliable internet and a quiet space. Ask your dietitian how they would like to receive your readings, whether by email or during the consult itself.</p>
<h2 id="what-blood-glucose-targets-will-your-dietitian-work-towards">What blood glucose targets will your dietitian work towards?</h2>
<p>Target ranges are set by your treating team and vary individually, and your dietitian works within those targets. A dietitian does not set diagnostic thresholds or replace your doctor&#8217;s advice on treatment targets.</p>
<p>The oral glucose tolerance test thresholds used to diagnose gestational diabetes in Australia are fasting 5.1 mmol/L or more, 1-hour 10.0 mmol/L or more, and 2-hour 8.5 mmol/L or more, based on the <a href="https://doi.org/10.2337/dc09-1848">international consensus criteria</a>. These are diagnostic cut-offs, not the same as the day-to-day targets your team will give you after diagnosis.</p>
<p>Follow your clinic&#8217;s advice on the ranges to aim for. Your dietitian uses those targets to shape your eating pattern and review your readings with you. A target is a target, so the odd reading outside it is information, not failure.</p>
<h2 id="how-can-you-get-the-most-out-of-your-first-gdm-dietitian-appointment">How can you get the most out of your first GDM dietitian appointment?</h2>
<p>Bring your oral glucose tolerance test results, any blood glucose readings you have, current medications, photos of your supplement bottles and a list of questions. Arm me with as much information as you want to arm me with, and no stress if some of it comes later.</p>
<p>Expect an individualised eating plan, practical meal and snack ideas and supplement guidance where appropriate. Ask about how often you will review and how to get in touch between appointments, because blood glucose needs often shift during pregnancy.</p>
<p>A Medicare rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan. Check with your GP about whether you qualify.</p>
