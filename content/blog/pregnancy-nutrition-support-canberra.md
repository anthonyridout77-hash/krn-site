## What does a pregnancy dietitian actually do?

A pregnancy dietitian is an Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) who helps you eat well for you and your baby from preconception through each trimester and into the postpartum months. We look at what you are eating now, any symptoms like nausea or reflux, your bloods and supplements, and build a plan that fits your real life.

## Where to start: public and private support

If you are booked into a public antenatal service, ask your midwife whether a dietitian is available. Hospital dietitians usually see women with a specific need, such as gestational diabetes, and you’ll generally need a referral from within the service.

Private dietitians can see you without a referral, at a time that suits you, and from preconception right through to postpartum. That continuity is why many women choose private care, either locally or by telehealth.

## Can I see a pregnancy dietitian by telehealth from Canberra?

Yes. Video consults work really well for pregnancy nutrition. We can go through your results and supplements, your usual week of eating and any symptoms, all from your couch. No parking, no waiting room, and no rushing out of work between scans.

## When should I see a pregnancy dietitian?

Preconception is the window most often skipped, ideally a few months before trying, so your folate, iodine and iron are in good shape ([Healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/planning-for-pregnancy)). Early pregnancy is a good time if nausea is making eating hard. Gestational diabetes, usually picked up at 24 to 28 weeks, is a very common reason to book ([Healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/gestational-diabetes)).

The postpartum months are often overlooked too. If your own meals have slipped while you look after everyone else, that’s really normal, and it’s a great time to get some support.

## Food safety in pregnancy

Most pregnancy food safety comes down to listeria: be careful with pre-prepared salads, deli meats and soft cheeses, and reheat leftovers until steaming hot. [Food Standards Australia New Zealand](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/) has the full list.

## How to choose a pregnancy dietitian in Canberra

Look first for the Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) credential. You can check it through [Dietitians Australia](https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/). It only takes a minute, and it tells you the person has a recognised dietetics degree and keeps up ongoing professional development.

Next, look for an evidence-based approach. A good fit will explain the why behind their advice and talk about overall eating patterns rather than hero foods, detoxes or expensive protocols. If something comes with a big promise about outcomes, that promise is usually running ahead of the evidence.

Then think about what matters for you. If you have gestational diabetes, PMOS (previously called PCOS), severe nausea, a vegetarian diet or a twin pregnancy, look for someone who works with these regularly. Ask how often they see this, and whether they will talk to your doctors.

Finally, the practical things count. How long is the first consult? Do you get a written plan? Can your partner come along? Is telehealth an option for reviews? These small details make a big difference to how supported you feel.
