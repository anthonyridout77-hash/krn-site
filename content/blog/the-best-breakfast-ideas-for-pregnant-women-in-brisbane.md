Starting the day with a wholesome breakfast is essential for everyone, but it is especially important during pregnancy. Eating the right foods helps support your growing baby and keeps your energy levels steady throughout the morning. If you’re wondering what to include in your morning meals, this guide will share some nutritious breakfast ideas tailored for expectant mothers.

Katrina Ridout, an Accredited Practising Dietitian & Nutritionist based in Brisbane, recommends focusing on nutritious recipes for pregnancy that provide essential nutrients like protein, fibre, healthy fats, and important vitamins and minerals.

### Why Breakfast Matters During Pregnancy

During pregnancy, your body requires additional nutrients to support the development of your baby. Eating a balanced breakfast can help regulate blood sugar levels and may even reduce morning sickness. Since many expectant mothers also experience being hormonal when pregnant, choosing the right foods can help manage mood swings and maintain overall wellbeing.

### Nutrient-Packed Breakfast Ideas

Here are some healthy and easy-to-make pregnancy dishes for your morning routine:

1. **Greek Yoghurt with Fresh Fruit and Nuts**

Greek yoghurt is a great source of protein and calcium, both important during pregnancy. Add fresh berries or sliced banana and a handful of nuts for healthy fats and fibre. This combination supports digestion and keeps you feeling full longer.

2. **Wholegrain Toast with Avocado and Poached Egg**

Wholegrain bread provides complex carbohydrates, while avocado offers heart-healthy fats. A poached egg adds a boost of protein and essential nutrients like choline, which is important for baby’s brain development.

3. **Porridge with Seeds and Fruit**

Oats are rich in fibre, which helps prevent constipation, a common concern in pregnancy. Top your porridge with chia or flax seeds for omega-3 fatty acids, plus fresh or dried fruit for natural sweetness.

4. **Smoothie with Spinach, Banana, and Protein Powder**

Smoothies can be a quick and nutritious option. Use spinach for iron and folate, banana for potassium, and a pregnancy-safe protein powder to keep you energised.

### Foods to Avoid

Alongside these healthy options, it’s crucial to be aware of things you **can’t** eat while pregnant to protect your health and your baby’s development. Avoid raw or undercooked seafood and eggs, unpasteurised dairy products, certain deli meats, and excessive caffeine. Katrina Ridout, a [pregnancy dietitian](https://www.katridout.com.au/our-services/pregnancy-nutrition-and-dietetics/) in Brisbane, advises expectant mothers to always check food labels and ask questions about preparation methods when dining out.
