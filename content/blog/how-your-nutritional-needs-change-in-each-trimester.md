## Pregnancy is a remarkable journey, not just emotionally but physiologically. As your body grows and adapts to nurture new life, your nutritional needs evolve to support both you and your baby.

Understanding how these needs change across the three trimesters, and how pregnancy hormones influence appetite, digestion, and metabolism, can make a significant difference to your health and wellbeing.

### First Trimester

The first trimester is a time of rapid development. Even before many women realise they’re expecting, vital organs and neural structures are forming. Key nutrients such as folate, iodine, and iron are essential in these early weeks. Morning sickness and nausea, however, can make eating challenging.

Hormones in pregnant women, particularly rising levels of oestrogen and human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), are often responsible for those familiar waves of nausea and food aversions. Small, frequent meals and bland foods can help manage symptoms. Foods rich in folate (leafy greens, citrus, wholegrains) and iron (lean red meat, legumes) support early growth and development.

As a pregnancy dietitian in Brisbane, Katrina Ridout advises focusing on practical, gentle nutrition strategies, listening to your body and nourishing it as best you can during this unpredictable phase.

### Second Trimester

By the second trimester, many women experience renewed energy and improved appetite. This is when nutritional demands begin to increase noticeably. Protein becomes particularly important for your baby’s growing tissues, and calcium helps strengthen developing bones and teeth.

You may also notice a change in digestion due to increased progesterone, one of the key pregnancy hormones that relaxes smooth muscle tissue throughout the body. This can slow digestion and lead to heartburn or constipation. Increasing fibre from fruits, vegetables, and wholegrains, along with plenty of fluids, can ease these symptoms.

During this stage, Katrina encourages balanced, nutrient-dense meals: lean proteins, colourful vegetables, wholegrains, nuts, and seeds. Omega-3 fatty acids from fish like salmon or sardines are also valuable for brain and eye development.

### Third Trimester

In the final trimester, your baby is rapidly gaining weight, and your body is preparing for labour and breastfeeding. Iron requirements peak during this period to support increased blood volume and oxygen transport. Zinc, vitamin C, and protein help strengthen your immune system and repair tissues.

Hormones in pregnant women, such as relaxin, increase flexibility in muscles and ligaments, while progesterone and oestrogen continue to fluctuate, sometimes affecting sleep and appetite. Eating smaller, nutrient-rich meals throughout the day can help manage discomfort and maintain steady energy levels.

At this stage, hydration is also crucial. Water supports amniotic fluid levels and helps regulate temperature. Electrolyte-rich foods like fruit, yoghurt, and soups can assist in maintaining balance, particularly in warmer climates like Brisbane.
