If you are doing IVF in Melbourne and wondering whether you need a local dietitian, telehealth makes that question a lot simpler. I work with women and couples all over Australia from Brisbane, and the support looks the same on a screen as it does in my clinic room. Nutrition sits alongside your fertility clinic’s care, never in place of it, and no diet can promise a result.

*By Katrina Ridout, Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD), Brisbane. Reviewed October 2026.*

## What does an IVF dietitian do before and during a cycle?

Before a cycle, I look at your overall eating pattern, any nutrient gaps, your supplements and your bloods, and we build a plan that gives you the most useful changes in the time you have. During a cycle, the aim shifts to keeping you nourished and steady, without adding new rules to everything else.

I guess the key thing is that this is a supporting role. Your fertility specialist leads the treatment, and I make sure food and supplements fit with it.

## Why does nutrition matter for fertility?

Eggs and sperm develop over roughly three months, so what you eat in the months before a cycle is what they grow in. The research is largely observational and points to overall eating patterns, especially Mediterranean-style eating, rather than single foods ([Gaskins and Chavarro](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajog.2017.10.003)).

What nutrition can’t do is make a cycle work, or increase the number of eggs you have. Being honest about that matters, so nobody walks away thinking a failed cycle was a failure of diet. It wasn’t.

## Which nutrients and foods are worth your attention before IVF?

Folate and iodine supplements are standard Australian advice before pregnancy ([Healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/planning-for-pregnancy)). Then I look at iron, especially with heavy periods or little red meat, vitamin D, B12 if you are vegetarian or vegan, and omega-3s from fish two to three times a week.

On the plate, that looks like half a plate of veg at lunch and dinner, wholegrains, legumes, nuts and seeds, a handful of berries, and protein at every meal. Simple little switches, not a whole new diet.

## How is male fertility nutrition different?

The principles are the same, but the conversation often focuses more on alcohol, weight, activity and overall diet quality. Antioxidant-rich foods like vegetables, fruit, nuts and wholegrains are a good baseline. I love seeing couples together, because it’s so much easier when you are both on board.

## How does PMOS or endometriosis change things?

With PMOS (previously called PCOS), insulin resistance is common, so pairing carbs with protein and fibre and spreading them across the day is often the starting point. The [2023 international PCOS guideline](https://doi.org/10.1093/humrep/dead156) supports healthy eating and activity as first-line care, with no single diet favoured over another.

With endometriosis, gut symptoms are common, and a low FODMAP approach done with a dietitian may ease them, although it doesn’t treat the condition.

## How to choose an IVF dietitian in Melbourne

Look first for the Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) credential. You can check it through [Dietitians Australia](https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/). It only takes a minute, and it tells you the person has a recognised dietetics degree and keeps up ongoing professional development.

Next, look for an evidence-based approach. A good fit will explain the why behind their advice and talk about overall eating patterns rather than hero foods, detoxes or expensive protocols. If something comes with a big promise about outcomes, that promise is usually running ahead of the evidence.

Then think about what matters for you. If you have PMOS, endometriosis or a thyroid condition, or you’re doing egg freezing or donor cycles, look for someone who knows those areas. Ask how often they see this, and whether they will talk to your doctors.

Finally, the practical things count. How long is the first consult? Do you get a written plan? Can your partner come along? Is telehealth an option for reviews? These small details make a big difference to how supported you feel.
