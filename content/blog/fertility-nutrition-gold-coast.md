## What does a fertility dietitian actually do?

A fertility dietitian is an Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) who works with women and couples in the months before conception, and through fertility treatment. The first consult covers your health and cycle history, any diagnoses, your bloods, your supplements and how you actually eat. From there you get an individual plan, not a generic list.

## How long before conceiving should you start on nutrition?

Ideally around three months, because eggs and sperm take about that long to develop. Folate and iodine supplements are recommended before you fall pregnant ([Healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/planning-for-pregnancy)), and the [NHMRC](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/health-advice/alcohol) advises that not drinking is safest when you are planning a pregnancy.

If you have less time than that, don’t stress. Starting where you are is still worthwhile.

## What can nutrition realistically change, and what can’t it?

Nutrition can make sure you are not going into pregnancy or treatment depleted, close nutrition gaps like low iron or vitamin D, steady your blood sugar if you have PMOS (previously called PCOS), and support egg and sperm quality through an overall Mediterranean-style eating pattern ([Gaskins and Chavarro](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajog.2017.10.003)).

What it can’t do is increase the number of eggs you have, or make a cycle work on its own. Anyone promising that is selling something. I’d rather be honest with you from the start.

## Medicare rebates and referrals

You don’t need a referral to book. If your GP sets up a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan, a Medicare rebate may be available for eligible patients, and private health extras may also contribute depending on your cover.

## How to choose a fertility dietitian on the Gold Coast

Look first for the Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) credential. You can check it through [Dietitians Australia](https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/). It only takes a minute, and it tells you the person has a recognised dietetics degree and keeps up ongoing professional development.

Next, look for an evidence-based approach. A good fit will explain the why behind their advice and talk about overall eating patterns rather than hero foods, detoxes or expensive protocols. If something comes with a big promise about outcomes, that promise is usually running ahead of the evidence.

Then think about what matters for you. If you have PMOS, endometriosis, a thyroid condition, or you are heading into IVF, look for someone who works in those areas. Ask how often they see this, and whether they will talk to your doctors.

Finally, the practical things count. How long is the first consult? Do you get a written plan? Can your partner come along? Is telehealth an option for reviews? These small details make a big difference to how supported you feel.
