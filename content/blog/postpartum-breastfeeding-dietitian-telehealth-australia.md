## Why telehealth suits the months after birth

Getting out of the house with a newborn is a project. There is the feed, the nappy, the car seat, the second nappy. By the time you arrive, you have forgotten half your questions.

Telehealth takes all of that away. You join a video call from wherever you are. If your baby needs a feed halfway through, you feed. If they need settling, we pause. I am a mum of three, so none of that bothers me at all.

Nutrition after birth is mostly about conversation and planning. I do not need to examine you to help with what you eat. I need your story, your bloods if you have them, and an honest picture of what a day looks like right now. That works just as well on a screen. If you are in the first weeks, my guide on [what to eat after birth when you have no time to cook](/blogs/what-to-eat-after-birth-no-time-to-cook/) is a good place to start.

## What to look for in a postpartum dietitian

If I were choosing someone for myself, this is what I would check.

- **The APD credential.** Accredited Practising Dietitians are university-trained and recognised by Dietitians Australia. You can confirm it on their find a dietitian directory.
- **Regular postpartum and breastfeeding work.** Ask how much of their week is spent with new mums.
- **A gentle approach to weight.** The early months are about recovery and feeding. Be cautious of anyone who pushes strict dieting while you are breastfeeding.
- **Respect for your other carers.** Your GP, midwife, child health nurse and lactation consultant all have a role. A good dietitian fits in with them.
- **Clear information about rebates before you book.**

## What a dietitian helps with after birth

Every woman is different, but these are the things I see most often.

- **Energy and tiredness.** Blood loss at birth and low stores from pregnancy are common reasons for exhaustion. If iron is an issue, read my article on [postnatal iron and energy](/blogs/postnatal-iron-and-energy-still-exhausted/), and ask your GP about a blood test.
- **Eating enough while breastfeeding.** Breastfeeding uses extra energy, and hunger is normal. We work out meals and snacks you can manage one-handed.
- **Iodine and other nutrients.** The NHMRC recommends a 150 microgram iodine supplement each day while breastfeeding. If you have a thyroid condition, check with your doctor before starting one. We also look at iron, calcium, vitamin D, omega-3 and, for vegetarian or vegan mums, vitamin B12.
- **Milk supply worries.** Supply is driven mainly by frequent, effective feeding. No single food is proven to increase it. I make sure you are fed well, and a lactation consultant helps with feeding itself. My [breastfeeding nutrition guide](/blogs/breastfeeding-nutrition-and-milk-supply/) covers this in more detail.
- **Baby with suspected food allergy or reflux.** If your doctor has suggested removing foods from your diet, I help you do it without missing out on key nutrients.
- **After gestational diabetes.** You will usually need a follow-up glucose test, and it is a good time to set up eating habits for the long term.
- **PMOS (previously called PCOS).** Hormones, appetite and energy can all feel different after birth. We tailor a plan around that.
- **Gentle weight changes, when you are ready.** We go slowly so energy, mood and supply are protected.

## Working alongside your care team

Nutrition supports your care and never replaces it. Your GP, midwife, obstetrician, child health nurse and lactation consultant stay at the centre. With your permission, I can send a summary to your GP so everyone is working from the same page.

Please contact your GP, midwife or hospital straight away if you feel unwell, have a fever, heavy bleeding, or feel low or anxious most days. Those are never questions to save for a dietitian. If you are worried about your baby's feeding or weight gain, speak with your child health nurse or GP too.

**Referral.** You do not need a referral to see a dietitian privately.

**Private health.** Most private health extras cover APDs, including telehealth with many funds. Check your own policy and limits with your insurer.

Current fees are confirmed when you book. Ask on the free call if you would like me to walk you through it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Brisbane dietitian help me if I live in another state?

Yes. I see women across Australia by telehealth video. We cover the same ground as an in-person consult, and your medical care stays with your local team.

### Can I have my baby with me during the appointment?

Yes, please do. Feed, settle or pace the room as you need. We can pause at any time.

### Do I need a referral?

No referral is needed to book privately. If you want to explore a Medicare rebate, talk to your GP first. A rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan, and the GP decides eligibility.

### Is it safe to lose weight while breastfeeding?

Gradual change is usually fine once feeding is settled and you feel ready. Strict restriction can affect your energy, mood and supply, so I keep things gentle and focus on eating well first.

### Do I still need a supplement after birth?

If you are breastfeeding, the NHMRC recommends 150 micrograms of iodine a day. Other nutrients depend on your diet and your bloods. Your written supplement plan covers what suits you.

### Is telehealth covered by private health insurance?

Many funds cover dietitian telehealth consults on extras, but policies differ. Check with your insurer before your appointment.
