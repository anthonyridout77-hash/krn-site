**Quick answer:** Choose an **Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD)** who specialises in fertility. Check that they assess your medical history, blood tests and cycle before writing a plan, that they work alongside your GP or fertility clinic, and that they can also support your partner. A good personalised plan fits your food preferences, culture, budget and schedule, not a generic PDF. Avoid anyone who promises a pregnancy or sells a supplement range.

*By Katrina Ridout, Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD), Brisbane. Reviewed September 2026.*

## 5 things to check

1. **Credential:** APD status, found in the Dietitians Australia directory. See [dietitian vs nutritionist](https://www.katridout.com.au/blogs/dietitian-vs-nutritionist-pregnancy/).
2. **Specialisation:** most of their caseload is preconception, fertility and pregnancy, including PMOS (previously called PCOS), endometriosis, male fertility and IVF.
3. **Assessment first:** they review bloods (iron, vitamin D, B12, thyroid, glucose), medications and your cycle before writing a plan.
4. **Team approach:** they’re happy to share notes with your GP or fertility specialist.
5. **Follow-up:** plans change as life does. Regular reviews over 3–6 months usually work better than a one-off plan.

## What a personalised fertility meal plan should include

* An eating pattern built around the evidence, mostly Mediterranean-style
* A plan written around your foods, culture, budget and cooking time
* An individualised supplement plan (folic acid, iodine and anything your tests show you need)
* Strategies for your condition, for example blood-glucose steadying for PMOS, or an anti-inflammatory focus for endometriosis
* Guidance for your partner
* IVF timing, if relevant. See [IVF dietitian support](https://www.katridout.com.au/blogs/ivf-dietitian-brisbane-nutrition/).

## Red flags

* Promises of a pregnancy or guaranteed results
* Selling their own supplements or “protocols”
* Extreme restriction, detoxes or very low-calorie diets
* No questions about your medical history

## FAQ

**Can I just download a fertility meal plan online?**  
Generic plans ignore your bloods, medications and preferences. They’re a starting point at best.

**How many sessions will I need?**  
Most people benefit from an initial consult and 3–4 reviews over around 3 months, the time eggs and sperm take to mature.

**Is telehealth as good as in person?**  
For fertility nutrition, yes. Most of the work is conversation, planning and follow-up.
