Starting IVF is one of the most significant decisions a person can make. Most women preparing for IVF focus intensely on what their clinic recommends — which injections to take, when to have scans, what the protocol looks like. Nutrition is often an afterthought. But the evidence is clear: **what you eat in the 90 days before egg retrieval, and throughout your cycle, meaningfully influences the outcome.**

This guide covers the nutritional priorities at each phase of an IVF cycle, based on current evidence and clinical experience as a [fertility dietitian](https://www.katridout.com.au/our-services/nutrition-for-fertility/).

## The Foundation: What to Eat Before You Start IVF

The 90 days before egg retrieval is the most critical nutritional window. This is the period during which the eggs that will be retrieved are developing (folliculogenesis), and the nutritional environment they develop in directly affects their quality.

The **Mediterranean dietary pattern** is the most consistently evidence-supported eating style for IVF outcomes. Some observational research suggests that closely following a Mediterranean-style eating pattern may be associated with somewhat better IVF outcomes, such as clinical pregnancy and live birth rates, though the evidence in this area is still mixed and continues to develop. This pattern emphasises:

* Abundant vegetables and fruit
* Whole grains over refined carbohydrates
* Legumes (lentils, chickpeas, beans) as a regular protein source
* Oily fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel) 2–3 times per week
* Olive oil as the primary fat
* Moderate dairy
* Limited red meat, processed meat, and ultra-processed food

## Key Nutrients for IVF Success

### Folate / Methylfolate

Essential for DNA synthesis and cell division — both core processes in egg maturation and embryo development. Most IVF clinics recommend folate supplementation, but women with MTHFR gene variants should take methylfolate (5-MTHF) rather than folic acid. Start at least 3 months before egg retrieval. Food sources: dark leafy greens, legumes, avocado, eggs.

### CoQ10 (Coenzyme Q10)

Mitochondrial antioxidant that supports cellular energy production in developing eggs. Multiple RCTs show CoQ10 supplementation (200–600mg/day) improves egg quality, fertilisation rates, and embryo quality in IVF — particularly in women over 35 and those with diminished ovarian reserve. Start supplementing at least 90 days before retrieval.

### Omega-3 Fatty Acids (DHA + EPA)

DHA is a structural component of the egg cell membrane and is essential for the acrosome reaction during fertilisation. Higher DHA levels in follicular fluid are associated with better egg quality and embryo development. Aim for oily fish 2–3 times per week plus a fish oil supplement providing at least 1g DHA+EPA daily.

### Vitamin D

Women with sufficient vitamin D levels (>75 nmol/L) have significantly higher clinical pregnancy rates in IVF than deficient women. Vitamin D receptors are expressed in ovarian cells, where they regulate follicular development and progesterone production. Have your levels tested — most women need more than the standard 1000 IU/day to achieve optimal levels.

### Iron

Adequate iron stores are essential for ovulation and early embryo development. IVF stimulation and egg retrieval place additional iron demands on the body. Have ferritin checked before starting — a ferritin below 30 μg/L warrants treatment before proceeding with IVF.

### Zinc and Selenium

Zinc is critical for egg maturation and fertilisation; selenium is part of the antioxidant system protecting follicular fluid. Both are commonly suboptimal in Australian women. Found in oysters, red meat, Brazil nuts, and eggs.

## Nutrition During IVF Stimulation

During the stimulation phase (typically 10–14 days of injections), your ovaries are working hard to develop multiple follicles simultaneously. Priorities during this phase:

* **Hydration** — critical for follicular development and to reduce risk of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS). Aim for 2–2.5L of water or clear fluids daily. If you’re at higher OHSS risk (women with PCOS, high antral follicle count), your clinic may recommend electrolyte drinks.
* **Adequate protein** — supports follicle development and helps maintain fluid balance. Aim for at least 1.2–1.5g per kg body weight daily. Include protein at every meal.
* **Continue antioxidant foods** — colourful vegetables, berries, olive oil, oily fish. These continue to protect developing eggs from oxidative damage right up until retrieval.
* **Avoid alcohol completely** — even moderate intake during stimulation has been associated with poorer egg quality and lower live birth rates.

## Nutrition Around Egg Retrieval

You’ll typically fast from midnight before retrieval. Afterward, focus on:

* **Protein and iron-rich foods** to support recovery
* **High-sodium foods and electrolyte drinks** if at OHSS risk (to maintain fluid balance)
* **Anti-inflammatory foods** — ginger, turmeric, berries, leafy greens
* **Avoid intense exercise and alcohol** in the recovery days

## Nutrition for Embryo Transfer and the Two-Week Wait

The research on specific foods for implantation is less robust than for egg quality, but the following principles apply:

* **Continue the Mediterranean pattern** — no need to change dramatically from what you’ve been doing
* **Adequate protein** supports the hormonal environment needed for implantation
* **Limit ultra-processed foods** — high processed food intake is associated with systemic inflammation that may impair implantation
* **Selenium and zinc** — continue supplementing to support early embryo development
* **Omega-3s** — continue; DHA in particular supports early embryonic development
* **Avoid raw or undercooked meat, raw fish, unpasteurised dairy** — standard food safety applies from transfer onward

## What About Pineapple Core After Transfer?

You’ve probably seen this circulating in IVF communities. The theory is that bromelain in pineapple core has anti-inflammatory properties that support implantation. The evidence is anecdotal — no RCTs support this practice. It’s unlikely to harm, but also unlikely to meaningfully change your outcome. Focus on evidence-based changes instead.

## Starting IVF: When Should You See a Dietitian?

Ideally 3–6 months before your first retrieval, so there’s time to address any nutritional deficiencies and optimise egg quality before the cycle begins. If your first cycle is imminent, a dietitian can still provide meaningful support for nutrition during stimulation, retrieval recovery, and the transfer phase.
