## What a fertility supporting plate actually looks like

Most of the fertility nutrition advice online is either a supplement list or a list of foods to fear. The evidence points somewhere far less dramatic. A Mediterranean style pattern is the one most consistently associated with better outcomes: vegetables and fruit across the day, wholegrains rather than refined ones, legumes, oily fish a couple of times a week, olive oil, nuts, and eggs for choline.

The three months before conception matter because that is roughly how long an egg takes to mature. It is not a window to panic about. It is a window where steady, ordinary changes are worth more than a perfect fortnight.

## Gestational diabetes does not mean cutting carbohydrates

Of everything I see, a gestational diabetes diagnosis lands the hardest. Women arrive convinced they have done something wrong. They have not. It is a placental hormone story far more than a dietary one, and it happens to women who eat beautifully.

The management is more forgiving than most people expect. You are not cutting carbohydrates out. You are choosing the type, spreading the amount across the day, and pairing them with protein so your levels stay steadier. Timing does more work than restriction does. Most women I support get their readings where they need them without the joyless eating they were braced for.

## Postpartum, the part nobody plans for

Everyone prepares for the birth. Almost nobody prepares for the eight weeks after it. Recovery, and breastfeeding if you are doing it, ask a great deal of your body at exactly the point you have the least capacity to plan meals.

What helps then is not a meal plan. It is food that works with one hand, batched where possible, and attention to the things that quietly run low after birth, iron in particular. That work is individual, which is why it needs a conversation about your bloods and your actual days rather than a template.
