## Telehealth from Brisbane, care that fits Adelaide life

I want to be upfront. I am not in Adelaide. I am a Brisbane dietitian, and I see Adelaide women by video call. For nutrition, that works well. I do not need to examine you to help with what you eat. I need your story, your bloods, your scans if they are relevant, and an honest picture of your week.

Telehealth also means no parking, no waiting room and no time off work to drive across town. You can join from home, from your car on a lunch break, or from the couch with a cup of tea. If you are trying to conceive, going through IVF, or pregnant and tired, that matters. My [fertility and preconception service](/our-services/nutrition-for-fertility/) and [pregnancy service](/our-services/pregnancy-nutrition-and-dietetics/) explain what we cover.

## What to look for in a fertility or pregnancy dietitian

Whether you see someone in Adelaide or online, here is what I would check.

- **The APD credential.** Accredited Practising Dietitians are university-trained and recognised by Dietitians Australia. You can confirm it on their find a dietitian directory.
- **Regular fertility and pregnancy work.** Ask how much of their week is spent with women trying to conceive or who are pregnant.
- **Plans built on your bloods and history.** A generic meal plan is easy to find online. A plan based on your results is not.
- **A careful approach to supplements.** More is not always better. You want someone who checks what you already take before adding anything.
- **Respect for your medical team.** Your GP, fertility specialist, obstetrician and midwife stay in charge of your medical care.
- **Clear information about rebates before you book.**

## What a dietitian does for fertility and pregnancy

Nutrition is one part of the picture. It supports the care you get from your doctors and never replaces it. Here is where I help most.

**Before conception.** We look at folate, iodine, iron, vitamin D and B12, and the overall pattern of what you eat. The NHMRC recommends a folic acid supplement of at least 400 micrograms a day for at least one month before conception and through the first three months of pregnancy, plus 150 micrograms of iodine a day. If you have a thyroid condition, check with your doctor before taking iodine.

**During IVF.** Treatment cycles are busy and emotional. I help you eat in a way that is simple, nourishing and realistic around appointments and medications. My article on [IVF nutrition support in Adelaide](/blogs/ivf-nutrition-support-adelaide/) goes into more detail.

**PMOS (previously called PCOS).** Many women with PMOS want support with cycles, insulin resistance and energy while trying to conceive. We build a way of eating that you can keep up.

**During pregnancy.** Nausea, food aversions, low iron, constipation, food safety and eating well on a tight budget all come up. If you develop gestational diabetes, I work with your diabetes team on a plan that fits your targets.

**For partners.** Sperm health is linked to diet and lifestyle too. Partners are welcome to join the call.

## Working alongside your Adelaide care team

You keep your local GP, fertility clinic, obstetrician and midwife. I do not replace any of them. With your permission, I can send a summary of your plan to your GP so everyone is working from the same page.

If you have pain, bleeding, fever, severe vomiting, or you feel unwell in any way, contact your GP, clinic or hospital straight away. Those are never questions to save for a dietitian.

## Fees and rebates

**Referral.** You do not need a referral to see a dietitian privately.

**Medicare.** A rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan; the GP decides eligibility. My [Medicare rebate guide](/blogs/medicare-rebate-dietitian-australia/) explains how it works, and it applies the same way in South Australia.

**Private health.** Most private health extras cover APDs, and many funds include telehealth consults. Check your policy and limits with your insurer.

Current fees are confirmed when you book. Ask on the free call if you would like me to walk you through them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Brisbane dietitian help me if I live in Adelaide?

Yes. I see Adelaide women by telehealth video. We cover the same ground as an in-person consult, and your medical care stays with your local team.

### Do I need a referral from my GP or fertility clinic?

No referral is needed to book privately. If you want to explore a Medicare rebate, talk to your GP first. A rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan, and the GP decides eligibility.

### Will you talk to my fertility clinic or obstetrician?

With your permission, I can send a summary to your GP. Your doctors stay in charge of your medical care, and nutrition fits in around their plan.

### Can my partner join the consult?

Yes. Partners are welcome on the video call, and we can talk about their nutrition too.

### Is telehealth covered by private health insurance?

Many funds cover dietitian telehealth consults on extras, but policies differ. Check with your insurer before your appointment.

### What do I need for my first appointment?

Recent blood results if you have them, a list of supplements and medications, and a rough idea of what you eat on a normal day. A quiet spot with good internet helps.
