## Why PMOS needs its own fertility conversation

PMOS can affect your cycle, your insulin response, your energy and how you feel in your own body. When you are trying to conceive, all of that lands at once. Then the internet hands you a list of foods to cut.

I am a dietitian and a mum of three, and I spend my working week with women planning a pregnancy. What I see most with PMOS is not a lack of effort. It is too much effort pointed in the wrong direction. Women arrive after months of strict eating, tired and unsure what to believe.

The 2023 international PCOS guideline supports healthy eating and regular movement for everyone with the condition. It does not name one diet as the right one for all women. That matters, because it means your plan should be built around your life, your bloods and your food preferences. A printed sheet cannot do that.

If you want the longer version of how diet fits in, read my article on [seeing a dietitian for PMOS when you want to get pregnant](/blogs/dietitian-pcos-pmos-getting-pregnant/).

## What to look for in a PMOS dietitian in Melbourne

You have plenty of options, so here is what I would check if I were choosing for myself.

- **The APD credential.** Accredited Practising Dietitian is the credential to look for. You can confirm it through the Dietitians Australia find a dietitian directory.
- **Regular fertility work.** Ask how much of their week is PMOS, preconception and IVF. PMOS care before pregnancy is different from general weight or diabetes advice.
- **Willingness to work with your team.** Your dietitian should be happy to write to your GP, obstetrician or fertility specialist.
- **No restriction by default.** Be cautious of anyone who starts by removing whole food groups without a clear medical reason.
- **Honesty about supplements.** Ask how they decide what you need, and what they would leave out.
- **Clear information about costs and rebates before you book.**

A dietitian who says "it depends on your bloods, let me look" is usually giving you a more useful answer than one who has a protocol ready before they have met you.

## What a dietitian actually does for PMOS and fertility

In our first consult I look at the whole picture. That means your cycle history, recent blood tests, medications, how you eat on a normal Tuesday, sleep, stress, movement and where you are up to with your doctor or clinic.

From there we choose a few priorities. Common ones include:

- Regular meals that keep your energy steady, built around protein, fibre and carbohydrates you enjoy
- Enough iron, iodine and folate before conception, with folic acid and iodine supplements in line with Australian advice
- Checking whether medications such as metformin mean your vitamin B12 should be reviewed by your GP
- Working out which supplements make sense for you, and which ones you can stop paying for
- Gentle, realistic changes if weight is part of the conversation, without shame and without crash dieting

Inositol comes up in almost every PMOS consult. The evidence is still developing, and the international guideline treats it as something to discuss individually. I go through it in detail in [inositol and PMOS: what a dietitian looks at](/blogs/inositol-pmos-pcos-dietitian-brisbane/).

Food cannot promise a pregnancy, and I will never tell you it can. What good nutrition care can do is help you feel well, reduce the guesswork and make sure your body is well nourished while you and your doctors work on the medical side.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Brisbane dietitian really help me if I live in Melbourne?

Yes. Telehealth video lets me review your history and blood results and build your plan with you from anywhere in Australia. Your medical care stays with your Melbourne doctors.

### Do I need a referral?

No referral is needed to book privately. If you want to explore a Medicare rebate, talk to your GP first, as a rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan and the GP decides eligibility.

### Do I have to give up carbs or dairy for PMOS?

Not by default. The international guideline does not recommend one specific diet for everyone. Carbohydrates can absolutely stay on your plate, and we choose the types and timing that suit you.

### Should I take inositol while trying to conceive?

That is an individual decision. It depends on your symptoms, your medications and your treatment plan. Please talk to your doctor or dietitian before you start, particularly if you are having fertility treatment.

### When should I start seeing a dietitian before trying to conceive?

Three to six months ahead gives us room to make changes gradually and check your nutrients. Starting later is still worthwhile. We simply work with the time you have.
