<p>I get asked about cost all the time, usually in a whisper at the end of a discovery call. So let&#8217;s talk about it plainly. Dietitians in Australia set their own fees, so there is no single national price, and I don&#8217;t publish fees in my articles. What I can do is show you the ways the cost can come down, so you walk into your first appointment knowing what to ask. I guess the key thing is this: a little bit of planning before you book can make a real difference to what you pay.</p>
<p><em>By Katrina Ridout, Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD), Brisbane. Reviewed October 2026.</em></p>

<h2 id="why-is-there-no-single-answer-to-what-a-dietitian-costs">Why is there no single answer to what a dietitian costs?</h2>
<p>Dietitians in Australia work as private practitioners and set their own consultation fees. Dietitians Australia does not issue fee guidelines for the profession, according to <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/seeing-dietitian/help-costs">Dietitians Australia</a>. Session length, how complex your situation is and how much coordination happens with your GP or specialist can all change what an appointment involves.</p>
<p>The credential matters too. The Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) program is the only dietetic credential recognised by the Australian Government for Medicare and Department of Veterans&#39; Affairs purposes, according to <a href="https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/healthcare-and-services/roles-and-specialties/dietitians">healthdirect</a>. Before you book anyone, check they hold APD status, because that decides whether you can claim a rebate at all.</p>
<p>The only figure worth relying on is the one the practice confirms with you before you book. It is completely fine to ask, and a good practice will tell you straight away.</p>
<h2 id="what-happens-in-a-first-dietitian-appointment">What happens in a first dietitian appointment?</h2>
<p>A first appointment usually runs 60 to 90 minutes, according to <a href="https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/healthcare-and-services/roles-and-specialties/dietitians">healthdirect</a>. That longer session is where your dietitian takes a full history, looks at your bloods and supplements, understands your goals and starts building a plan that fits your life. Review appointments are usually shorter and more focused.</p>
<p>Telehealth and in-person care involve the same time, preparation and expertise, so many practices treat them the same way. Telehealth can still save you travel, parking and time off work, which counts for a lot when you are juggling a job or a little one.</p>
<h2 id="does-medicare-cover-dietitian-appointments">Does Medicare cover dietitian appointments?</h2>
<p>Sometimes. Medicare may cover part of some dietitian visits when your GP has put a Chronic Condition Management Plan in place and referred you, according to <a href="https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/healthcare-and-services/roles-and-specialties/dietitians">healthdirect</a>. Without that plan and referral, the appointment is privately funded.</p>
<p>Even with a plan, the rebate reduces the cost rather than removing it, and you pay the gap, according to <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/seeing-dietitian/help-costs">Dietitians Australia</a>. Ask the practice what your out-of-pocket amount will be before you book, and confirm your eligibility with your GP.</p>
<p>The number of rebated allied health visits under a care plan is limited each calendar year, according to <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/seeing-dietitian/help-costs">Dietitians Australia</a>. That number is shared across all your allied health providers, so it is worth mapping out who you are seeing before the year runs away from you.</p>
<h2 id="can-private-health-insurance-dva-or-ndis-help-with-the-cost">Can private health insurance, DVA or NDIS help with the cost?</h2>
<p>Yes, several pathways can help. Most private health funds cover visits to APDs in private practice, according to <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/seeing-dietitian/help-costs">Dietitians Australia</a>. Check your extras cover, your annual limit and any waiting periods before you book, because funds vary a lot in what they include.</p>
<p>The Department of Veterans&#39; Affairs may cover APD costs for holders of a Veteran Gold Card, or a Veteran White Card where treatment is for an accepted service-related condition, according to <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/seeing-dietitian/help-costs">Dietitians Australia</a>. NDIS participants may also be able to access dietetic support through their plan.</p>
<p>Some government compensation schemes may cover APD visits as well, including Comcare, WorkSafe Queensland, WorkSafe Victoria, TAC in Victoria and Return to Work South Australia, according to <a href="https://dietitiansaustralia.org.au/seeing-dietitian/help-costs">Dietitians Australia</a>.</p>
<h2 id="do-you-need-a-referral-to-see-a-dietitian">Do you need a referral to see a dietitian?</h2>
<p>No. You can book a private dietitian without a referral. A referral only becomes relevant when you are claiming a Medicare rebate under a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan, according to <a href="https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/healthcare-and-services/roles-and-specialties/dietitians">healthdirect</a>.</p>
<p>A referral is also how your GP and dietitian stay in the loop with each other. That shared picture helps your care, whether or not you are claiming a rebate.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-plan-your-appointments">How to plan your appointments</h2>
<p>The per-visit fee is only part of the picture. Ask any dietitian how they structure reviews before you start, so you know roughly how often you will be seeing them and what that looks like over a few months. If you have a care plan, line your rebated visits up against your private health extras so you get the most out of both.</p>
<p>A few questions worth asking before you book:</p>
<ul>
<li>Are you an Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD)?</li>
<li>What will my out-of-pocket amount be for the first appointment and for reviews?</li>
<li>Do you offer telehealth, and is it handled the same way as in-person visits?</li>
<li>How often do you usually see people for reviews?</li>
</ul>
