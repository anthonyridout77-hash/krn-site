Preeclampsia is a pregnancy complication characterised by high blood pressure and signs of damage to other organ systems, often the kidneys. It typically occurs after 20 weeks of pregnancy and can pose serious risks to both the mother and baby if not managed properly. Understanding preeclampsia and adopting appropriate lifestyle and dietary strategies is essential for expectant mothers.

Katrina Ridout, an Accredited Practising Dietitian & Nutritionist, emphasises that diet plays a significant role in supporting maternal health during pregnancy, especially when managing conditions like preeclampsia.

## What is Preeclampsia?

Preeclampsia involves a sudden increase in blood pressure, which can lead to complications such as organ damage, premature birth, or restricted foetal growth. Symptoms may include swelling, headaches, and visual disturbances, although sometimes it develops silently. Early diagnosis and management are crucial.

## The Role of Diet in Managing Preeclampsia

While there is no single food or diet that can cure preeclampsia, maintaining balanced nutrition can support overall pregnancy health and may help reduce complications. Pregnant women should focus on wholesome, nutrient-rich foods and avoid certain items that can exacerbate symptoms.

## Nutritional Focus Areas

#### Balanced Sodium Intake

Controlling salt intake is often recommended to manage blood pressure. However, completely eliminating sodium is not advised during pregnancy. Katrina Ridout suggests moderation, using natural herbs and spices to enhance flavour instead of excessive salt.

#### Rich in Antioxidants and Fibre

Foods high in antioxidants, such as colourful fruits and vegetables, can support vascular health. Wholegrains, pulses, and fibre-rich foods help maintain steady blood sugar levels and support digestion.

#### Adequate Protein

Protein supports foetal growth and repair of body tissues. Lean meats, dairy, nuts, and pulses are recommended as part of daily meals.

#### Hydration

Staying well-hydrated is important, as dehydration can worsen blood pressure. Drinking plenty of water throughout the day is encouraged.

## Dietary Approaches by Katrina Ridout

As a dietitian Brisbane trusts for pregnancy nutrition, Katrina Ridout provides practical guidance on managing diet with preeclampsia:

* Emphasising pregnancy dishes that incorporate fresh vegetables, lean proteins, and wholegrains.
* Offering nutritious recipes for pregnancy designed to support both mother and baby while keeping blood pressure stable.
* Advising on things you can’t eat while pregnant, including excessive caffeine, unpasteurised dairy, and processed foods high in salt and sugar.

Katrina also highlights the importance of listening to your body, especially during times of being hormonal when pregnant, which can affect appetite and food preferences. Working with an Accredited Practising Dietitian & Nutritionist can help tailor meal plans that meet both nutritional needs and personal comfort.
