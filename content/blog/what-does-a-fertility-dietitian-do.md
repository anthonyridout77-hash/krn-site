If you’re trying to conceive — naturally or through IVF — you’ve probably heard that nutrition matters. But what does a **fertility dietitian** actually do, and how is seeing one different from just eating well or following generic advice online?

As an Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) specialising in fertility nutrition, I’m often the first port of call for women and couples who feel like they’re doing everything right but still not getting results. Here’s what working with a [fertility dietitian](https://www.katridout.com.au/our-services/nutrition-for-fertility/) actually looks like.

## What Is a Fertility Dietitian?

A fertility dietitian is an APD who specialises in reproductive nutrition — the branch of nutrition science that covers how what you eat affects your hormones, egg quality, sperm health, cycle regularity, and your ability to conceive and sustain a pregnancy.

In Australia, the title “dietitian” is legally protected. Only those who have completed an accredited university degree and are registered with Dietitians Australia can call themselves an APD. This matters in fertility, where there’s an enormous amount of misinformation — from Instagram supplements to fad protocols with no scientific basis.

## What Does a Fertility Dietitian Actually Do?

### 1. Assess your nutritional status and diet quality

Before making any recommendations, a fertility dietitian takes a full dietary assessment — looking at micronutrient adequacy (folate, iodine, iron, zinc, vitamin D, omega-3s), meal timing, dietary patterns, and any deficiencies impacting hormone production or egg quality.

### 2. Interpret your bloodwork and medical history

A fertility dietitian works alongside your GP, gynaecologist, or fertility specialist to interpret relevant blood results — including thyroid function, iron studies, AMH, and hormonal markers — and translate these into actionable nutrition changes.

### 3. Create a personalised fertility nutrition plan

There’s no single “fertility diet.” A woman with PCOS needs a different approach to someone with endometriosis, and both differ from someone preparing for IVF. A fertility dietitian tailors recommendations specifically to you.

### 4. Review and optimise your supplements

The supplement industry is overwhelming. A fertility dietitian cuts through the noise, recommending evidence-based supplements at therapeutic doses — and weeding out expensive, unnecessary ones. This includes preconception multivitamins, folate vs. methylfolate, CoQ10 for egg quality, vitamin D dosing, and more.

### 5. Support you through IVF cycles

Nutrition before and during an IVF cycle can influence egg quality, embryo development, and implantation. A fertility dietitian will adapt your plan to each phase — stimulation, retrieval, transfer, and the two-week wait.

### 6. Address underlying conditions affecting fertility

Conditions like PCOS, endometriosis, thyroid disorders, and insulin resistance all have nutritional components that can be addressed to improve fertility outcomes. Nutritional treatment works alongside — not instead of — medical care.

## When Should You See a Fertility Dietitian?

The short answer: sooner than you think. Nutritional status in the 3–6 months before conception significantly impacts egg and sperm quality, implantation success, and early fetal development.

You should consider seeing a fertility dietitian if you:

* Are trying to conceive naturally and want to optimise your chances
* Have been trying for 6+ months without success
* Have been diagnosed with PCOS, endometriosis, unexplained infertility, or low AMH
* Are about to start an IVF or FET cycle
* Have had a miscarriage and want to understand if nutrition played a role
* Are unsure which prenatal supplements to take
* Have a health condition (thyroid disease, coeliac disease, insulin resistance) affecting fertility

## Is a Fertility Dietitian Covered by Medicare?

Yes — in Australia, dietitian consultations may be partially covered under a **Medicare Chronic Disease Management (CDM) plan**. If your GP identifies a chronic condition — such as PCOS, endometriosis, or insulin resistance — they can refer you to a dietitian for up to 5 Medicare-rebated sessions per calendar year. Private health insurance also commonly covers dietitian consultations under extras cover.
