## What gestational diabetes actually is

During pregnancy, the placenta makes hormones that make insulin work less well. Most bodies make extra insulin to keep up. Some cannot keep up enough, and blood glucose rises higher than is healthy for pregnancy.

That is gestational diabetes. It is common. It is not caused by one bad week of eating, and it is not your fault.

I am a mum of three and I have worked as a dietitian for more than 20 years. I have seen how much worry this diagnosis brings. The good news is that food is one of the most useful tools you have, and you do not have to work it out alone.

## What to look for in a Melbourne dietitian

Whether you see someone in person or by telehealth, these are the things I would check.

- **APD credential.** Accredited Practising Dietitians are listed in the Dietitians Australia find a dietitian directory.
- **Regular pregnancy work.** Ask how often they support women with gestational diabetes.
- **Works within your team's targets.** Your blood glucose targets come from your doctor or clinic. A dietitian should help you reach them, not swap in their own.
- **Keeps carbohydrate in your diet.** You and your baby need it. Be wary of advice to cut it out.
- **Looks at your real readings.** A good plan changes as your numbers change.
- **Clear about costs before you book.**

## Hospital dietitian or private dietitian?

Many Melbourne women are cared for through a public hospital antenatal or diabetes clinic, and some will see a hospital dietitian there. If that is offered to you, please go. It is connected to the people looking after your pregnancy.

Seeing a private dietitian can add something when you:

- Are waiting a few weeks for your first clinic appointment
- See a private obstetrician who does not have a dietitian on the team
- Eat vegetarian, follow cultural food traditions, or are managing nausea or food aversions
- Work shifts or have little ones at home and meals are all over the place
- Want more time to understand what your readings are telling you

Let your hospital team know you are seeing someone privately, so everyone is on the same page.

## What a dietitian does for gestational diabetes

In our first consult I go through your glucose test results, your readings so far, any medications and supplements, and what a normal day of eating looks like for you. Then we turn that into a plan you can actually live with.

We usually work on:

- Spreading carbohydrate across meals and snacks
- Pairing carbohydrate with protein, fibre and healthy fats
- Choosing carbohydrate types and portions that suit your readings
- Breakfast, which is often the hardest meal of the day
- What a high fasting reading might mean, and what to ask your team
- Snacks for work, school pick-ups and busy afternoons
- Making sure you still eat enough for you and your baby

You can get a head start with my [gestational diabetes meal plan guide](/blogs/gestational-diabetes-meal-plan-guide/).

Some women also need metformin or insulin. That is not a sign you did something wrong. As pregnancy goes on, hormones can push glucose up no matter how carefully you eat. Medication decisions sit with your doctor, and we adjust your eating plan to fit.

The way gestational diabetes is diagnosed in Australia changed in 2025, and services have moved across at different times. I explain this in [why the gestational diabetes test numbers changed](/blogs/gestational-diabetes-test-numbers-changed-adips-2025/). Ask your Melbourne team which criteria they use.

## Working with your Melbourne care team

Your obstetrician, midwife, GP, hospital clinic or diabetes educator stays in charge of your medical care. With your permission, I can send them a short summary so everyone knows what we are working on.

Please contact your doctor, midwife or hospital straight away if you feel unwell, if your readings fall outside the range they have asked you to report, or if you notice a change in your baby's movements. Those are never questions to save for a dietitian.

Your diabetes educator or doctor can register you with the National Diabetes Services Scheme. This gives you access to subsidised testing supplies and good information.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Brisbane dietitian help me if I live in Melbourne?

Yes. By telehealth video I can review your readings and results and build a plan with you from anywhere in Australia. Your medical care stays with your Melbourne team.

### Do I need a referral to book?

No. You can book privately without one. If you would like to look into a Medicare rebate, talk to your GP first. A rebate may be available for eligible patients on a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan, and the GP decides eligibility.

### Do I need to cut out carbs?

No. Carbohydrate is an important part of eating well in pregnancy. We focus on the type, the amount and the timing across your day.

### What should I bring to my first telehealth consult?

Your glucose test results, your blood glucose readings, a list of medications and supplements, and any targets your team has given you. If some of it is not ready, that is fine.

### What happens after my baby is born?

For most women glucose levels return to normal after birth. You will usually be asked to have a follow-up glucose test, and your risk of type 2 diabetes later in life is higher, so regular check-ups with your GP matter.
