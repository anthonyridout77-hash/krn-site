This is the breakfast I suggest most often in pregnancy, for one simple reason: it is made the night before, so the version of you who is tired and queasy in the morning does not have to make any decisions.

By Katrina Ridout, Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD), Brisbane. Reviewed November 2026.

It takes about four minutes of actual work, it keeps for three days in the fridge, and it is built around fibre rather than around a supplement.

## The dietitian's note

The adequate intake for dietary fibre in the Australian and New Zealand Nutrient Reference Values rises from 25 grams a day for adult women to 28 grams a day in pregnancy, and to 30 grams a day while breastfeeding. Most people do not add that extra three grams, because pregnancy is usually when eating gets harder rather than easier.

This recipe is designed with that gap in mind. Rolled oats, chia seeds and a whole pear with the skin left on each contribute fibre, and because the oats and chia soak overnight they arrive with a good deal of fluid already attached, which matters more than people expect. Fibre and fluid do their work together.

It is general education rather than individual advice, and it is not a treatment for anything. If you are uncomfortable and it is not shifting, that is a conversation for your GP, midwife or an Accredited Practising Dietitian. There is more on the detail in [constipation in pregnancy: what an Australian dietitian looks at first](/blogs/constipation-pregnancy-dietitian-brisbane/).

## Ingredients

Makes one serve. Scale it up and make three at once, which is what I do.

- 1/2 cup rolled oats
- 1 tablespoon chia seeds
- 2/3 cup milk of your choice
- 2 tablespoons natural yoghurt
- 1 small ripe pear, skin on, roughly chopped
- 1/4 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1 tablespoon chopped walnuts or almonds, optional
- A drizzle of honey or maple syrup, optional

## Method

1. Put the oats, chia seeds and cinnamon in a jar or a small bowl and stir them together while they are dry. This stops the chia clumping, which is the only thing that can go wrong here.
2. Add the milk and the yoghurt. Stir properly, right to the bottom.
3. Add about two thirds of the chopped pear and stir it through.
4. Cover and refrigerate overnight, or for at least four hours.
5. In the morning, top with the remaining pear, the nuts if you are using them, and a drizzle of honey or maple syrup if you want it sweeter. If it has thickened more than you like, loosen it with a splash of milk.

## Notes and swaps

- **Prep time:** about 4 minutes. **Chill time:** at least 4 hours, ideally overnight. **Keeps:** 3 days, covered, in the fridge.
- **If pears are not in season**, a grated apple with the skin on works the same way, as does a cup of mixed berries.
- **If you are off dairy**, use a fortified soy milk, which gets you closer on protein and calcium than most other alternatives.
- **If nausea is the problem**, leave out the nuts and the honey and serve it cold and plain. Cold, bland and already made is usually the combination that gets eaten.
- **If reflux is the problem**, make a smaller jar and have it as two smaller serves rather than one large one.
- **Make it ahead**, three jars at a time, on a Sunday night. The pear is better added fresh if you are going past the second day.

## Why it works on a weeknight

There is no cooking, no blender and no washing up beyond one jar and one spoon. Everything in it is a cupboard or fridge staple, which means it survives the week when the plan does not.
