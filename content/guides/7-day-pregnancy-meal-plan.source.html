Foods to Avoid During Pregnancy:

Before we begin, let's talk about which foods you should avoid to keep your tiny bubba safe…

Pregnancy changes your immune system, making you more susceptible to foodborne illnesses such as listeriosis, salmonellosis and toxoplasmosis, which can cause serious problems for you and your baby. So it's important to avoid foods with a higher risk of carrying harmful bacteria or parasites. This advice follows Food Standards Australia New Zealand.

  1. Deli Meats and Undercooked Meat - Sliced cold meats, pâté and raw or undercooked meat, chicken or fish can carry listeria or salmonella. Eat meat freshly cooked and steaming hot.
  2. Soft Cheeses and Unpasteurised Dairy - Brie, camembert, ricotta, feta, blue cheese and unpasteurised milk can carry listeria. Hard cheeses and pasteurised milk and yoghurt are fine.
  3. Raw or Runny Eggs - Raw or undercooked eggs, including homemade mayonnaise and aioli, can carry salmonella. Cook eggs until firm.
  4. Raw Seafood and High-Mercury Fish - Avoid sushi, raw oysters and cold smoked salmon. Limit shark (flake), swordfish, marlin, orange roughy and catfish, which are high in mercury.
  5. Pre-Cut Salads and Fruit - Pre-packed salads, salad bars and pre-cut fruit can carry listeria. Wash and cut your own.

Foods to Eat During Pregnancy:

Now, I think that's enough talking about what you shouldn't eat during pregnancy… now let's talk about what you should aim to include.

Pregnancy increases your body's need for nutrients such as folate, iodine, iron, calcium and protein, which are essential for your baby's growth and development.

Eating a balanced, nutrient-rich diet supports a healthy pregnancy and helps you feel your best. It can also help establish good eating habits that benefit both you and your baby in the long term.

  1. Dark Leafy Greens - Spinach, kale and silverbeet are rich in folate, a B vitamin that helps form your baby's neural tube, the start of the brain and spine.
  2. Lean Protein Sources - Lean meat, chicken, fish, eggs and legumes help grow and repair your baby's new tissues and make important hormones, enzymes and antibodies for their immune system.
  3. Wholegrains - Wholemeal bread, rolled oats, brown rice and quinoa are rich in fibre, B vitamins and folate, and help keep constipation at bay.
  4. Dairy Foods - Pasteurised milk, hard cheese and yoghurt are rich in calcium, which is important for your baby's bones and nervous system.
  5. Berries - Strawberries, blueberries and raspberries are rich in antioxidants and vitamin C, which also helps your body absorb iron.

Quick Tips to Follow During Pregnancy:

Remember, it's not only about what you eat and don't eat during pregnancy. Here are some important tips to keep you feeling your best during your pregnancy journey:

  1. Eat small, frequent meals throughout the day to help with nausea, dizziness and heartburn.
  2. Drink plenty of fluids, mostly water and milk. Keep caffeine under 200 mg a day and avoid alcohol.
  3. Limit foods and drinks that are high in added sugar, salt and saturated fat.
  4. Take folic acid (400 micrograms) and iodine (150 micrograms) daily, plus anything else your doctor recommends.
  5. Talk to your dietitian about any dietary restrictions or concerns you may have.
