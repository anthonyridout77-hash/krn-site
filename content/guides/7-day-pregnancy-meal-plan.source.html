Your 7-Day Pregnancy Meal Plan

A week of simple, Australian meals and snacks that follow current food safety advice for pregnancy. Real portions, a snack pick-list and a shopping list, so you can stop second-guessing and just eat.

Katrina Ridout, Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD008207) · Brisbane and telehealth Australia-wide

Start here

How to use this plan

This is a guide, not a set of rules. Swap days around, repeat the meals you like, and use leftovers. Every day gives you three meals and two or three snacks, because eating regularly helps with energy, nausea and steady blood glucose.

Hungrier some days?

That is normal. Your needs change through pregnancy. Most women need little or no extra food in the first trimester, a little more in the second, and a bit more again in the third. Add a snack from the pick-list when you are hungry. You do not need to "eat for two".

Less hungry or nauseous?

Eat smaller amounts more often. Dry crackers, toast, cold foods and plain carbohydrate are often easier. Page 9 has more ideas. If you cannot keep food or fluids down, see your GP or midwife.

What a day looks like across the food groups

The Australian Dietary Guidelines suggest these serves a day for pregnant women aged 19 to 50. The plan is built around them.

Vegetables5 serves (1 serve = ½ cup cooked veg or 1 cup salad)
Fruit2 serves (1 serve = 1 medium piece or 2 small pieces)
Grains8½ serves, mostly wholegrain (1 serve = 1 slice bread, ½ cup cooked rice or pasta, ½ cup cooked oats)
Protein foods3½ serves (1 serve = 65 g cooked meat, 80 g cooked chicken, 100 g cooked fish, 2 eggs, 1 cup legumes, 30 g nuts)
Dairy or alternatives2½ serves (1 serve = 1 cup milk, 200 g yoghurt, 40 g hard cheese, or a calcium-fortified plant milk)

Supplements most women need

  • Folic acid 400 micrograms a day, from at least one month before pregnancy through the first three months. Some women need a higher dose, so check with your GP.
  • Iodine 150 micrograms a day through pregnancy and breastfeeding. If you have a thyroid condition, ask your doctor first.
  • Iron, vitamin D and others only if your blood tests or your doctor say so.

Keep you and your baby safe

Food safety in pregnancy, the short version

Pregnancy changes your immune system, so infections like listeria and salmonella are more serious. Every meal in this plan already follows the advice below, based on Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ).

AvoidDeli and sliced cold meats, pâté, pre-cut or pre-packed salads and fruit, soft and semi-soft cheeses (brie, camembert, ricotta, feta, blue) unless cooked until steaming, raw or runny eggs, raw seafood and sushi, cold smoked salmon, raw sprouts, unpasteurised milk and juice, soft-serve ice cream.
Fine to eatHard cheeses (cheddar, parmesan, tasty), pasteurised yoghurt and milk, eggs cooked until firm, canned tuna and salmon, freshly cooked meat, chicken and fish, commercial mayonnaise, fruit and vegetables you wash and cut yourself.
FishAim for 2 to 3 serves a week of lower-mercury fish (salmon, barramundi, canned tuna, sardines). Limit shark (flake) and billfish (swordfish, marlin) to 1 serve a fortnight with no other fish that fortnight. Limit orange roughy and catfish to 1 serve a week with no other fish that week.
LeftoversRefrigerate within 2 hours, eat within a day, and reheat until steaming hot all the way through.
DrinksNo alcohol is the safest choice. Keep caffeine under 200 mg a day (about 2 cups of instant coffee, or 1 café coffee). Water is your main drink.

Snack pick-list

Pick one between meals, or two if you are hungry. Each pairs fibre with protein so it keeps you going.

1 piece of fruit + 30 g nuts
170 g yoghurt + ½ cup fruit
4 wholegrain crackers + 40 g cheddar
Veg sticks + 3 tbsp hummus
1 slice wholegrain toast + 1 tbsp nut butter
2 hard-boiled eggs + cherry tomatoes
1 cup edamame
1 cup milk or a milk-based smoothie
½ cup roasted chickpeas
2 rice cakes + ¼ avocado

Still hungry after a meal?

Add another snack from the list, an extra slice of wholegrain bread, a glass of milk, or more vegetables. Hunger is a signal to listen to, not to fight.

When things get tricky

Nausea, reflux and aversions

  • Keep plain crackers by the bed and eat a few before getting up.
  • Eat small amounts every 2 to 3 hours. An empty stomach often makes nausea worse.
  • Cold foods smell less. Try yoghurt, fruit, sandwiches and smoothies.
  • Sip fluids between meals rather than with them.
  • For reflux, keep portions smaller, eat earlier in the evening and stay upright after meals.
  • If a food on this plan turns your stomach, swap it for anything from the same food group.

See your GP or midwife if you are vomiting many times a day, cannot keep fluids down, or are losing weight.
